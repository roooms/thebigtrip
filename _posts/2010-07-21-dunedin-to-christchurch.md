We woke up early and headed out of Kaka Point, it was good looking but not too much to do this time of year so we headed to Dunedin. After arriving around lunchtime we went straight to the i-site, I had found out that the annual Cadbury Chocolate Festival was in its last couple of days so we went to find out more. I picked up a flyer and saw that the famous Jaffa race had already started, we quickly went back to the van and found our way to Baldwin St which is the steepest street in the world according to the Guinness Book of Records.

The Jaffa race is the annual highlight of the festival and this year was in its tenth year, to celebrate they were racing 50,000 enlarged Jaffa's (orange, sugar coated, orange chocolate balls) half of them orange, half purple down Baldwin St. Unfortunately we arrived just as it was all finishing up but we did get to walk up the extremely steep street and check out all the failed Jaffa's. Most of them ended up in drains and bushes!

After that we bought some Jaffa's from a local shop to try out and went back into town for a proper look around. The city has a lot of stone buildings and although I've never been, it did have a real Scottish feel to it. I managed to get hold of a second hand copy of the book 'The Girl With The Dragon Tattoo' which I have been searching for ever since I was given the second one in the series back in the Cook Islands! We saw a nice looking burger bar but refrained and went to find a campsite for the night.

We woke up to a faint drizzle in the morning, deciding to stick to our original plan we went to a local farmers market to see what yummy delights we could pick up. Found some locally made cheese from a factory we were planning to stop at later in the day and also picked up some rolls.

Then we took a drive out onto the Otago Peninsula, this is attached to the mainland but juts out into the sea and is a hub for wildlife.

We drove all the way along to the end of the peninsula where we took a blustery walk to the Albatross centre, the tours were heavily discounted that day and due to this they were booked out. We went back out to the van and after a couple of minutes were rewarded with seeing one of the huge birds coming in from the sea. It circled above us a few times, I managed to catch a shot of it before it disappeared to land and tend to its chick. So cool to see one of these huge birds.

We decided that as the weather was not so good we would head further north and see how far we got. Took a stop on the way at the Moeraki boulders which are amazingly spherical rocks scattered on a beach, once I'd got some pics we carried on.

We ended up in Oamaru and found the Whitestone cheese factory where we stopped for a drink. Whilst in there we discovered quite a random fact. The cast of the TV programme 'Scrubs' used to get the Whitestone cheeses flown in for them whilst they were recording the series. There were loads of signed pictures of the cast dotted around the cafe!

After a much needed break we went to have a look around the historic district and found a load of people in period dress and a shop full of penny farthings. Next we went to have a look at where the Little Blue Penguin Colony was, we found out it was now $25 each to go and watch the penguins come in from the sea and you weren't allowed to take pictures. We decided against it, I would have liked to have seen them but we had already been lucky enough to see the Yellow-eyed Penguins for free. Dan got some pictures of crashing surf that was quite impressive and gathering a few spectators.

The next day we were up and off on a longish drive to Christchurch, once we arrived we went to find the Explore depot in town. The battery for our fridge in the van was no longer working so we had decided to take the van back early and get a refund for the last few days. Luckily they didn't have another van they could give us so agreed to the refund. We then had to empty the van which we had spread ourselves out in rather well over the last couple of months. We stuffed everything into any space we could find and I found us a hostel, Dan had to go and fill up the van with fuel and he noted that we had done 7,402 km then we got a taxi to our new home for the next few days.

It felt strange leaving the van and for the next few hours felt like we had forgotten something, however once we had lay down on our first proper bed for 51 nights we were glad we were out of it!

Foley Towers was only about a 15 minute walk into Christchurch city centre and had a really homely feel. It had lots of Basil Fawlty references dotted around but no Manuel!

On our first day after a nice lay in we took a walk into Christchurch and wandered around in the sun, went to check out all the familiar street names such as Gloucester, Worcester and Hereford! Found that we could get free wifi in the Library and had lunch in the Botanical Gardens.

The next day we went back into town, Dan researched and booked our bags to be shipped home and I went to find the Contemporary Lounge Ginger Meggs Hairdressers to get my roots sorted before my birthday. We went back to the hostel and unpacked all our bags, sorted through everything that we didn't need anymore and repacked the board bags till they were fit to burst, luckily we were getting charged by the size of the bags!

Wednesday was really lazy, we had to stay in and wait for the Courier to come and pick up our bags. When they arrived Dan sorted it out and I watched the van containing our bags drive away hoping that they would eventually get home ok! We treated ourselves to a really tasty lunch over the road at a place called the Herb Cafe then it was back to the comfy lounge where I finished the second book 'The Girl Who Played With Fire' great book!

So onto our last day in NZ, as it was all about using up our food we had a huge bowl of porridge with the last of our raisins and honey and then took a walk into town. I left Dan in the library and went to the salon, really nice light space and everyone was friendly. A girl called Di put my foils in and let me rough dry my hair off, no more roots! I was a bit nervous but it was all good, cheers Donna.

The weather was not great, same as it had been for the last few days so we decided to head back to the hostel, confirm our flight and pack our one bag each.

Off to hopefully a sunnier Australia which contain some of our lovely friends to hang out with, bring on the weekend!
