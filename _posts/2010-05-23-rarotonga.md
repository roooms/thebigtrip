We arrived at Rarotonga Airport in the dark just before sunrise. Walking out of the plane we were hit by the warm, still air, we entered the small airport and were serenaded by a local guy playing a Ukalele and singing welcoming songs to all us weary passengers.

We collected our luggage (including our snowboarding gear) and headed back out into the heat to meet Mano, our lift to the guesthouse. Mano patiently waited while we bugged a guy at the yet-to-open Air Raro desk to let us store our luggage for the next three weeks.

With everything sorted we headed off with Mano in a rickety van to the Maiana Guesthouse, our home for the next nine days. As we drove there the sun was coming up and I could just make out glimpses of water through the trees. We arrived at the guesthouse and before seeing our budget room we were shown a lovely bungalow right on the beach, it was double the price of our room in the house so we declined.

Our room turned out to be fine, very basic but we only had to sleep there. We had shared bathrooms, kitchen and living room as well as a garden full of fresh fruit and flowers and the beach was just across the road.

The sun had just come up and so we headed out to explore, we went straight down to the beach and walked along. It was gorgeous the whole way along, white sand in varying degrees of courseness, turquoise water dotted with coral and ringed by dark blue sea and crashing surf. Inland the beach was fringed by lush greenery and coconut trees and as you move more inland it becomes more like rainforest.

We walked all the way to Muri beach which is the most popular beach on the island and it's obvious why. The lagoon there is home to four Motu's (little islands) which you can swim, kayak or even walk (it's only waist deep) over to! So we spent the first few days here hiring a moped, Dan getting a Cook Islands driving licence! Finding our way around the Island and getting food for our stay, getting bitten by Mosquitos, woken up and annoyed by Roosters who have no idea what the time is and go off cock-a-doodle-doing at random times of the day and night!

We also met our housemates Heidi and Chris who were really nice and both German.

Unfortunately after the amazing weather on the first day it went a bit downhill with overcast days and random torrential downpours of the heaviest rain I have ever seen, it even woke me up! So we spent time in the house and pottered around on the bike, we went to see Wigmore Waterfall after a big downpour of rain as it gets more dramatic. We went in for a swim and there were a few boys there jumping in so Dan went for a slippy climb up the rock and jumped in a few times.

We also got followed by dogs everywhere we walked! Rarotonga is known as the Island of Dogs, most of them are owned but they just have the run of the island and if they decide to go for a walk with you, you don't get a choice and they just wander off when they have had enough. We saw them on the road, on the beach, swimming with their doggy mates in the sea, trying to catch fish in the lagoon and even actually catching one of the many Roosters on the island!

We had a cultural afternoon with Ina (Mano's wife and joint owner of the guesthouse) as the weather was still a bit rubbish. We sat on the balcony of one of the bungalows that Heidi was staying in and made Ei's which are headbands made out of coconut leaves and fresh flowers. Traditional plates out of coconut leaves and toys.

Ina disappeared and came back with piles of fresh fruit, papaya, passion fruit, mango, watermelon, coconut and grated coconut all on our plates we had made, yum!

Our only proper night out was at an Island night where we got traditional food and a show of Rarotongan dancing. It was a great night although it started strangely by a guy singing Barry White songs!

Then the drums and music started and three female dancers and four male dancers all dressed traditonally came onto the stage and did numerous different dances which all had meanings or told stories.

The dancing was high impact, fast paced and hypnotic. I was pretty much staring the whole time at the dancers especially the girls hips, they moved so quickly and I couldn't work out how!

The finale of the show was three of the guys husking coconuts with their teeth and then cracking them open on stage, very impressive. We all left on a high.

Spent the next couple of days sunbathing when the sun popped out, sorting out our trip to Aitutaki and Atiu and also had a trip to the local market.

On Sunday we went to Church with the family which also happened to be Mother's Day in Rarotonga. Everyone was really friendly and we were greeted warmly with kisses. The service was good, relaxed and they had a band who played songs and a projector with the words on so you couldn't get out of the singing!

After the service there was a whole spread of food put out for everyone, we were urged up by the family who were insistent that we fill our plates and wanted us to go back for seconds! The people here know how to eat!

Finally the sun came out and after having some time to chill out in it we went and did the cross island walk which goes from the north side to the south via the highest point on the island called the needle.

Well this turned out to be a bit more strenuous than just a "walk". It took us five hours and we had to climb up the side of hills using roots of trees as hand holds, practially had to slide down steep bits on our bums as there was no grip and spent about an hour guessing the route as we didn't see any markers.

However we made it and it was worth it, we tramped through rainforest up to an amazing lookout at a rock called the needle (with an inquisitive rock climbing Rooster). Wandered next to a stream that grew and eventually turned into the Wigmore Waterfall that we had visited a few days before.

It was a really good experience that left us shattered and we know for next time to have more water and food.

When we got back to the guesthouse we told our new housemates Sarah and Chris about it and said how tiring it was. They then told us about their trekking in Nepal and up to Everest base camp so I'm sure they will have no problem doing it!

The next day was our last on Rarotonga and it was just spent packing and getting to the airport. When we arrived and checked in we were given the seats 4A and 4G, I thought we would have been sat together so asked the lady and she said oh no it's ok there is only one seat on each side of the plane!

We boarded and took our seats on the fifteen seater prop plane, then one of the pilots gave us the safety info and we were off into the sky with a great view of Rarotonga and off to experience another Cook Island, Aitutaki.
