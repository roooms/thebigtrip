We landed at Auckland International Airport at around 9am Tuesday morning having left Rarotonga at 7am Monday on a 4 hour flight. Because our trip continues west we lose that day forever (or until we do a round the world trip flying east). I’d called from Raro and booked our first few nights accommodation in Parnell, a district within Auckland just south-east of the CBD. It was a place called [City Garden Lodge](http://www.citygardenlodge.co.nz/), a large wooden house built for Tongan royalty.

Nat had collected an Abalone shell in Mendocino, California which we had to declare on the way in to New Zealand. It is illegal to take Abalone shells from NZ so we wanted proof it had been brought in from outside the country so we wouldn’t have any hassle leaving with it. I declared I had mud on my boots from clambering around in the burial caves of Atiu and got them cleaned for free by customs.

Auckland’s airport is 23km south-west of the city centre so we had to book transport to get us to Parnell. We checked out the i-SITE visitors centre at the airport and were amazed at the amount of information and free guides we could take. We were advised it would be about a dollar more to get a shuttle direct to our destination with all our bags than get the airport bus to Central Auckland (the cheapest option) with a short hop on the Link bus that circles the outer districts. There were also free AA guides and maps for every inch of road in New Zealand. These would come in handy when we picked up the camper van.

We booked the shuttle and the driver helped us load our bags. We had to wait 12 minutes to see if anyone else wanted to join us (the shuttle bus rules), but nobody arrived so we left the airport in our private transport.

We arrived a bit early at City Garden Lodge and they were just finishing cleaning the rooms. We had a choice of three and settled on room 12 as it was the biggest and we had all our snowboarding gear. The lodge was setup perfectly for a house full of people. The kitchen was huge with three sinks and three hobs. A massive fridge full of cool bags with other peoples names on sat in one corner, and shelves of partly used spices and oils was in another, free to use for everyone.

We dropped of our luggage, had showers and ventured out toward Newmarket, billed as the fashion capital of New Zealand. We didn’t have the cash to stop in the designer shoe shops but found a good food court where we could get some lunch and picked up some groceries for the next few days in the supermarket. Back at the lodge we packed it away in the kitchen and headed deeper in to Parnell. It’s full of great cafés, bars and art galleries. At the north end the road winds in to Central Auckland and there were a number of camper van rental companies dotted along the route. We got one quote but the others were just shutting up for the day. We’d have to come back tomorrow.

That evening we cooked food at the lodge, checked goings on back home via email and Facebook, and posted a bit more to the blog. Tomorrow we were going to follow an itinerary suggested in the Lonely Planet book for New Zealand. Our bedroom was cold so we used hot water bottles.

Up reasonably early the next day we walked to the [Auckland Museum](http://www.aucklandmuseum.com/) and looked around the exhibits on pacific life and natural history. The Maori history area was really interesting and it had more meaning having come from the Cook Islands where we had seen the way of life still evident today. There were wildlife exhibits on native New Zealand birds like the [Kakapo](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakapo) and [Kiwi](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiwi), an ocean section with life size replicas of sharks and dolphins, and a large part of the museum dedicated to volcanoes. Feeling more educated, we left the museum without seeing the third floor (war history) as time was pressing on and we had lots more to do.

We walked through Auckland Domain, a large park the museum was built within, passed the city hospital and across Grafton Bridge to Karangahape Road (known locally as K Rd). This was the edgier side of the city with eccentric shops and a mix of different nationalities and cuisine. One thing we noticed about Auckland from this brief wander was the number of new and second-hand book shops it has. Many more than anywhere else we have been so far. We are both working our way through books pretty quickly and trading them at each new backpackers or hostel that has a bookshelf.

From K Rd we walked down through Myers Park, along Queen Street and up to the Auckland Art Museum. It boasted free admission in the LP book but couldn’t have predicted that on arrival we would be greeted with masses of construction work. It would re-open in June 2011. We decided we couldn’t wait that long and moved on. We walked through Albert Park and down along the side of Auckland University to the High Street. Nat had an address for an optometrists to get some more contact lens cleaner so we stopped in there before finishing our walk at the waters edge, where we would catch the ferry to Rangitoto tomorrow.

It was early enough to stop in another camper van rental place on the way home, so we walked back along Beach Road to the Parnell area, stopping for a bit in the [Lonely Dog Gallery](http://www.ivanclarkegallery.com/lonely_dog.html) where the lady behind the counter gave us the history of the author and mentioned a film was being made about the lonely dog. The artwork was great and we will be keeping an eye out for any more info on the film.

Our last stop was Backpacker Rentals where we got a great deal on a camper van for the next two months. As it is now considered the winter season the rates were really low for what will be our wheels and our bed. We caught the Link bus back up the hill through Parnell to our lodge as we were shattered from all the walking. The following day would be an earlier start as we had a ferry to catch.

Te next day we were running a bit late and rather than walking to the CBD had to catch the Link bus back to the Ferry Building to make sure we got on the 9.15am ferry to Rangitoto. The weather was looking a bit unsettled but we sat in the open air at the back of the ferry and took pictures of Central Auckland as we cruised away from it.

[Rangitoto](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangitoto_Island) is a volcanic island only 600 years old, the youngest to be formed in the bay and witnessed by a Maori community living just a few hundred metres away on a neighbouring island, Motutapu. We planned to hike to its summit, walk the rim of the crater and back down again to catch the 12.45pm ferry from Rangitoto to Devonport a small town on the opposite side of the water from Central Auckland. We made it to the top with only a few stops for water and had about forty minutes to enjoy the view which had improved dramatically as the cloud hugging the peak had cleared. Nat got busy with the camera taking pictures of Central Auckland back across the water, and of a cute species of bird called the Fan Tail.

It was quicker walking back down and we had chance to look at some preserved holiday homes (New Zealanders call them a Bach) that were by the waters edge as we waited for the ferry. It was colder on the short hop over to Devonport and once docked we were surrounded by a small gathering of Victorian and Edwardian houses and shops. It felt very otherworldly in contrast to the business district of Central Auckland but very welcoming. We bought some postcards, checked out another two or three book shops and took some pictures of the area. We stopped in a coffee shop for a quick snack and to write out the cards only to find the Post Office building across the street wasn’t a post office anymore. It did however have a very cool ‘Foods of Briton’ store inside selling all the things that would make us feel at home. Branston Pickle, Angel Delight, Party Rings biscuits, Twinning’s Tea and Marmite were all available at a premium for anyone who was feeling homesick (and wishing they still lived in the late eighties).

As if the days walking wasn’t punishing enough, we decided to walk back to Parnell from the Ferry Building. On the way back we decided we should treat ourselves to fish and chips and after a quick stop at the lodge we walked to Al & Pete’s, a fish and chip shop about five minutes from our accommodation. It finished the day off perfectly. Tomorrow we would collect the camper van and head north to Whangarei, home of [Zion Wildlife Gardens](http://www.zionwildlifegardens.co.nz/) and the TV series ‘The Lion Man’.
