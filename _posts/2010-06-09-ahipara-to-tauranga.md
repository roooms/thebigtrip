---
title: "Ahipara to Tauranga"
date: 2010-06-09 23:18:00
author: "Natalie"
---
We woke in Ahipara to a dewy, chilly but sun drenched morning. The sky was blue and would have been a perfect day to spend on the beach but we had to stick to our schedule and get to Tauranga in a few days so we headed off down the coast after one last look along Ninety Mile Beach.

Our drive South took us through lots of small towns and we crossed the Hokianga Harbour (fourth biggest in NZ) on a car ferry, and continued on until we reached the Waipoua Kauri Forest. This is the home of the Kauri Tree which at one time covered a huge area of the North Island. It is like the Giant Redwood's in America only these are slightly shorter and thicker.

We stopped off and took a couple of walks to see some of the larger trees. First we stopped to have a look at Tane Mahuta (named for the Maori Forest God), this tree is 51m high and has a 13.8m girth. Then we had a longer walk to look at the Four Sisters which are four trees fused together at the base.

Had an almost road-kill today. A duck type bird decided to take off in flight at the side of the road and into our direction. Luckily I was going quite slow and slowed down enough that he hit the windscreen and kept flying. All that happened was that I knocked the shit out of him, literally!

Our stop for the night was at a place called Baylys Beach near Dargerville, we didn't see much of it as we arrived in the dark and the next morning left in the wet! The good weather spell was broken and clouds covered the sky. We did a big drive down to a place called Matakohe and went to The Kauri Museum. They had all kinds of saws and machines which were used to cut down and chop up the Kauri trees when they were being logged and huge cross-sections of trees as well as a reproduction of the sawmill to show just how big the industry in Kauri trees was.

Back on the road we continued our big drive down to a place not too far from Auckland but on the west coast called Piha. This is a great black sand beach with an extremely rough sea and fantastic looking houses dotted into the steep backdrop. There are many warnings about going into the sea because of the dangerous rips, especially this time of year when there are no lifeguards but people still do because the surf is fantastic! There is even a reality tv show called Piha Rescue which is on over here and we saw in the Cook Islands.

When we arrived it had stopped raining but was really windy, we went down to the beach for a bit to watch all the crazy surfers in the rough and freezing sea.

Pete you would have loved it!

Our campsite was cute and had a Bowling Club right next door so we went in for a nice relaxing Corona and to catch up on the news which was on a big screen. It seemed like the only food place in little Piha and had a really nice homely atmosphere. We headed back to our van to cook up a stir-fry.

Had really heavy rain overnight which is here to stay for a bit if the weather man got it right last night.

We had planned to go for a walk along the beach but it was just too wet so we headed off on our journey to Tauranga where we would be camping for free for a couple of nights at a family friends house.

Jason is a mate of my Dad's and had lived in the UK for about twenty years before moving back to NZ a couple of years ago.

We did a quick stop in Auckland to pick up a couple of roll-mats from the depot where we got our van. Hopefully they will give us some extra padding!

Arrived in Tauranga in the afternoon and were welcomed into Jason's house. We had coffee and chatted about where we have been so far. Then Jason started cooking roast lamb for all of us and we cracked open a couple of bottles of wine. We chatted for hours and before we knew it the time had crept away and it was one in the morning, our latest night for ages!

It was really nice to be staying with someone familiar and was nice to have a bit of a break from traveling everyday. We went for lunch the next day with Jason, his Mum and Dad and Brother. It was the Queen's birthday which they have as a bank holiday in NZ so it was a day to catch up with the family, which included us for the day!

We had a great day and got loads of advise and also the offer of places to stay with various relatives all over the country.

That evening Jason took us to a place called Fernland Spa which is an outdoor pool filled with natural mineral water from a hot spring. It was so nice to just lay in the hot water and relax. A bit like a Jacuzzi though you can't spend too long in there before you start to overheat.

Back at the house we had a fantastic sausage curry whipped up for us and ended up having another late night discussing our route for the next few days.
