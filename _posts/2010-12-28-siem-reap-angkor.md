Our bus actually left on time and the journey was ok. We stopped a couple of times for snacks and were pleased to find our new favourite thing, bamboo holding cake. We had tried it at the school and consists of sticky rice cooked with coconut milk and soy beans then stuffed inside a piece of bamboo, very tasty and cheap!

We arrived in Siem Reap at 7pm and found or rather were found by a tuk tuk driver, we loaded our stuff into the cab and were off to our guesthouse for a bargain price of $2. When we arrived at Garden Village we checked out the room before checking in but it was fine and had a double and a single bed which would be good as Jenna was meant to be joining us the next day and we could share to cut the cost.

Back at the tuk tuk Dan was trying to pay our driver the $2 but he had now told us that he only said that price as he was banking on us booking him to take us to the temples of Angkor the next day. We had already decided that we wanted to ride around them on pushbikes so took his number in case we wanted to do a second day at some of the temples further out. It was quite frustrating being played like that but he is only trying to make a living and we would soon find out that everyone in Siem Reap is desperate for your business!

After dumping our bags we had a beer in the rooftop bar before going out to see what SR had to offer. The place was buzzing with tourists and had loads of bars and restaurants to cater for them, we decided to go for a street food option and went for bbq after our fab one in PP, we sat off the street at the usual plastic table and chairs.

The food was ok and we were far enough off the street not to be bothered too much by the people begging, of which there were a lot. Finally though a few kids came over selling books on Angkor and postcards, they were quite chatty and one was showing off his knowledge of England which was impressive but when asked he didn’t know the population of Cambodia!

I ended up buying some postcard from him as we needed some more but as soon as I did I was bombarded by questions by the two boys either side of me as to why I hadn’t bought their postcards earlier. I knew they were playing me as we had only just arrived and I told them so to which I was then given fake tears and sworn at repeatedly by one of them. We then decided it was time to go, we paid up and walked back to guest house through the gauntlet of kids and women with babies all begging. It is one of the hardest things to say no and keep walking especially when they are hanging onto you but we have given more in Cambodia than anywhere due to the amount of people affected by landmines and we give a bit everyday but would have nothing left if we gave to everyone. We have also read stories of people still working and providing for their families with injuries and lost limbs so we know that it can be done and that the people here see the tourists as an easier way to make money.

On the Saturday we were up early and left the guesthouse by 6am, we rode to Angkor which took about half an hour with a quick stop to buy our tickets. Our first sight that morning was the sun just above Angkor Wat and the mist rising up from the moat which surrounds it. We stopped to take it in whilst eating a breakfast baguette from a seller riding past. After parking our bikes we crossed the moat and were amazed at how quiet it was, little did we know at the time that luckily we had just missed the sunrise rush! We did a little offering and good luck prayer to Vishnu and then set off to explore the temple.

Angkor Wat is the heart and soul of Cambodia it is their national symbol. It is the biggest religious structure in the world and is the only one of the Angkor monuments which has been in continuous use since it was built back in the 12th century so it is in the best condition of them all.

It is set in a huge area of land and the temple itself is covered in carvings depicting stories and gods from Hinduism, especially Vishnu for whom it is built.

As we were leaving Angkor Wat there were hoards of people arriving and again we thanked our timing.

The next place on our route was the fortified city of Angkor Thom which is 10 sq km and at it’s height the city had a population of about 1 million whilst at the same time London had only 50,000! The city has five 20m high gates to enter through, we cycled through the South gate and were really impressed by the detailed sculpture. Once inside we visited the Bayon, this has 54 gothic towers which are decorated with 216 smiling faces. This was one of our favourite temples, the faces looked amazing it was as if they were looking down at you the whole time. There were some people there in traditional dress posing for photos for a small fee but we snapped some sneaky free ones! We cycled on to the Terrace of Elephants which is 350m long before taking a much needed break from the heat in one of the many food stalls literally fighting for your custom.

Back on the road we rode out of the North gate and to much calmer, quieter surroundings. The next temple we came to was Preah Khan which is one of the largest complexes at Angkor, sitting within a wall of around 700m by 800m it is thought to have been a Buddhist University. This was another favourite, it was more of a ruin than the others and because of that it had more atmosphere, it was also a lot quieter. There were trees battling with the brickwork, there were many carvings and a small old man crumpled into a corner next to a Buddha shrine giving out good luck strings. We accepted one each and he did a little prayer which was translated to us by another visitor as “good luck for your travels”. We left him a decent donation and moved on to our next destination feeling lucky.

After another few km’s we stopped briefly at a couple of temples called Ta Som and Eastern Mebon before carrying on to one called Pre Rup. This is meant to be a great spot to watch the sunset as it is quite high but we were there a few hours too early, Dan ran up the many step’s take some pics by himself as I was beginning to feel temple fatigue setting in and I wanted to save myself for the next big one!

On the way to the next place Dan had a blow out on his rear tyre, while we were checking it out a guy stopped and said not far up the road was a guy who could fix it. We were glad to be out of the saddle a bit and it wasn’t far to the place the guy had mentioned. Once there we found out the tyre had been previously patched up and the inner tube was broken too, it cost us $5 to get back on the road but would have been more to tuk tuk it home.

We finally arrived at Ta Prohm after dodging all the fruit and craft sellers and it was well worth the cycling. This is the temple which has been in many movies including Tomb Raider, it is slowly being overtaken by the forest that surrounds it and has many corridors blocked by collapsed walls or ceilings. I think by far my favourite it had an ancient feel to it and it was just fascinating to try to work out how the trees had grown up through the brickwork, the late afternoon sun was shining through the trees which gave the place a very ethereal feel.

Feeling slightly templed out we made our weary way home, stopping for sweet snacks on the side of the road. We had clocked up about 30km’s today which meant once we were back we had a bit of a nap before going up to the bar for much needed food and some Angkor beers!

Back in the room we were chilling out when we heard a familiar voice coming form outside, it was Jenna. She had just arrived from Phnom Penh, she checked into our room and we caught up before all getting an early night.

On Sunday we had a well deserved lay in before going to find a chilled out breakfast at a place called the Peace café. We booked our bus to Battambang and managed to do a bit of catching up online using the guesthouse wifi.

That evening we went to the old market where we saw our first plate of deep fried Tarantulas, there were so many of them all piled up and typically we didn’t have the camera! We decided against trying one and settled for some spring rolls instead, hoping they didn’t contain mushed up spiders.

Next we went to the night market which contained all the usual things we had been seeing and left once I had found my charm for Cambodia.

Back at the guesthouse we climbed the huge steps up to the bar where we found Jenna and her new travel buddy Christie. We exchanged stories of Angkor over our beers and some food. We stayed till the bar shut and made our way to the room, Jenna was off out to sample Siem Reap but we decided to give it a miss as we didn’t feel like venturing back out and we were feeling the pinch of the dollar.

In the morning we said goodbye to Jenna as she was off to Bangkok, we waited in reception for our minivan which was on time and deposited us at the big bus. We got comfy on the half empty bus and settled into a random Khmer comedy show which would hopefully put us to sleep.
