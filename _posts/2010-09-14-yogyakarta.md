We arrived in Yogyakarta, bypassed all the hassling becak drivers and headed off to the backpacker area using our trusty map. On the way we were befriended by a guy called Joni who led us to a few different Losmen (basic, homestay style guesthouses). In the end we settled for one called Losmen Lucy and thanked Joni wondering how much we were going to have to pay him for his services and he just said no problem and walked away. We felt bad for thinking that he would be expecting money but we only thought that because a large majority of the locals would. Such a nice feeling that someone helped us out for nothing, sometimes here you feel afraid to ask something in case it costs you!

We went for a wander around and it is a nice place, loads of touristy stuff for sale but didn’t see that many tourists. There were push bike becaks and horse and carts to get around in and loads of street food.

We went to an internet café to have a catch up and booked ourselves on a tour for the next day of two really important temples in the area called Borobudur and Prambanan.

Spent our evening eating very cheap street food sat on a straw mat and then looking at Batik Art which was being sold to benefit local street kids (apparently). Anyway we ended up buying one of the temple Borobudur which we would be seeing the next day so it had meaning and we both really loved it.

Went back to get some kip before our early start.

Woke up at 4.30am and went out to meet our bus driver, it was just getting light and was actually a really nice temperature to be wandering around the tiny gangs (streets) of Yogya. There were nine of us in the minibus which headed off to get to Borobudur before the crowds. As we went along we saw the sunrise over one of Java’s Volcanoes which was stunning.

We arrived at Borobudur around 6am and were sent off to climb the 9th century AD temple. It really was a great sight and even nicer being surrounded by mist and lush grounds against a backdrop of mountains. It was really quite quiet as we started our ascent and clockwise walking around the many terraces.

Borobudur is one of the world’s most important Buddhist temples and has 1500 carved pictures telling the story of Buddha and also 502 Buddha statues throughout.

The temple was really peaceful until about thirty school kids turned up ranging from ages 5 to 15 to practise their English on the tourists! They were all really good especially two little ones who were like a tag team, there must have been prises for whoever got the most signatures as these two were running around the whole time getting as many people to speak to them as possible. All we had to do was answer some questions that they had made up and then sign their books; the trouble was that a lot of the questions were the same and around every corner you were ambushed by small groups of children. We felt like we had a taste of celebrity lifestyle repeating ourselves and signing books.

We finally made it to the top of the temple and had a few minutes peace before we have to leave to get back to the minibus, this was like running the gauntlet past more students through a local market and finally back to the restaurant where we had just enough time to stop for breakfast. Back on the bus we all discussed our celebrity experiences.

The next stop was a quick one and not far from Borobudur, it was a place called the Mendut temple which houses a 3 metre high statue of Buddha. People say that this statue was intended to be placed on top of Borobudur but it was impossible to raise it to the summit.

Our journey to Prambanan took about an hour and we were all feeling the heat by the time we got out of the minibus. Walking around this series of temples wasn’t so much fun in the midday sun but I think the temples were a bit more spectacular.

Prambanan is the grandest Hindu temple in Java; it was also built in the 9th century AD and features 50 temple sites although there has been extensive damage due to an earthquake back in 2006.

There was a lot of restoration work going on and the temples that were still standing were very impressive. We were able to go inside some of them where we found statues of different Hindu Gods. The main Shiva Temple was closed due to it being unsafe which was a shame but it was still good to see the outside.

By the time we got back to the minibus I think we were all templed out, luckily it wasn’t a long trip back to Yogya where we went and chilled out for the afternoon.

That evening we went out to meet a couple we got chatting to earlier in the day called Matt and Caz, a guy called Hansel also joined us for some Bintang and banter. The evening carried on into the small hours where Dan and Matt found themselves playing (and losing) Badminton against some very talented local guys at about 3am.

Due to our late night the next day started late too. We woke up about half an hour before check out time so jumped up and got our stuff packed which I have to say we are getting really quite good at now!

Then we planned our day over breakfast, decided to go to the bus terminal to see if we could get a bus to Denpasar, Bali.

First we had to get on a local bus to get to the terminal which worked out fine, then we negotiated a ticket and paid what we had been told to and finally we were ushered onto a bus which left about five minutes later, timing!

Our journey to Bali was a long one but also pretty comfy, the bus we were on had totally reclining seats, a leg rest and lots of leg room. Also it was empty apart from one other passenger and three drivers who took it in turns to drive the 18 hours! Not too much crazy music this time and the driving was pretty safe, we slept lots and I even slept through the slightly rough ferry crossing to Bali which I was amazed by.

I woke up as we were docking, the sun was just coming up and people were starting their days, Bali style. We saw people working the beautifully landscaped rice paddies, children on their way to school, women all dressed up on their way to prayer to give the offerings they were skilfully balancing on their heads. We passed many decorated temples, evidence that we were no longer in a Muslim country but a predominantly Hindu one.

We were looking forward to experiencing what Bali had to offer.
