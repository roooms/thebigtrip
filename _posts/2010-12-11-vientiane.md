We arrived in Vientiane just as the sun was setting and took the usual action of jumping in a group tuk tuk to take us the extra few k’s into town, then we started our hunt for accommodation. The place we had been on the look out for was full and so were a lot of the others. We bumped into Vanessa, Sarah and Michelle from Luang Prabang and said we’d meet them on the front after we found a place.

After an hour we had found one a bit out of town but cheap and cheerful, dumped our bags with relief and went out to meet the girls.

We found them at an Indian restaurant so we grabbed the last free table outside and celebrated with a beer and a sweet lassi. The food was fab and we both had veggie curries in practice for India.

Feeling full we went with the girls to find the bowling alley which is recommended in the lonely planet as an ‘activity’. When we finally found it there was a hive of activity inside with lots of locals playing, some even had their own balls and were taking it quite seriously!

When there was an alley free we had a game and some beers. It was loads of fun and great to catch up with the girls again who were all off on their travels again the next day, said our goodbyes and headed back to our digs before the 11.30pm curfew!

Our next day was sightseeing day and boy did we pack it in. We hired pushbikes and headed off to find a place called COPE which is an organisation that supports the victims of UXO (unexploded ordnance) and helps them to get their lives back with artificial limbs. Unfortunately we couldn’t find it and after about 45 minutes of looking we had to move on.

Next up was the Wat Si Saket, this temple is the oldest in Vientiane and contains more than 2000 small Buddhas in niches and 300 seated and standing large Buddhas. It was really nice to see although I think we are almost over the temples especially as each one has a separate entry price for foreigners.

Our third place on the list was the Patuxai which is replica of the Arc Du Triomphe, it commemorates Lao who were killed in pre-Revolutionary wars and was built of cement that was donated by the USA and was meant for a runway, ha!

The final sight of the day was a bit of a peddle but worth it, a huge golden structure called Pha That Luang is the most important national monument in Laos. Legend has it that a piece of Buddhas breastbone is enclosed in part of the stupa.

That evening we took a walk along the river and to a huge statue of someone who is obviously very important then had a beer for him!

We had a very relaxed final day in the capital. After booking our sleeper bus tickets we spent the day wandering around some different areas and came across a nice little French style square where we spent a few hours catching up on email and writing postcards. We left the bakery we were in before they asked us to leave and walked quite a long way along the river to a little place for a beer and to watch the sunset over Thailand and the Mekong river.

We just had time to grab some food at the Indian place we had been to on the first night before getting back to our guesthouse and waiting for our pick up. When it arrived it was a packed tuk tuk van and we got the front seats, the driver managed to squeeze one more person before heading to the bus station. We both had our fingers crossed for a decent bus for the next 10 hours!
