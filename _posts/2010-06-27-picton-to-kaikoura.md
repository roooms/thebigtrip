---
title: "Picton to Kaikoura"
date: 2010-06-27 19:07:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
The ferry departed just before 8am. It was gloomy outside and Wellington didn’t look its best so instead of taking pictures we tried to find somewhere comfortable to sit for the next four hours. There was a lounge area with a TV about to start showing films but it smelt so bad that we settled for a café upstairs where a few of the doors led outside, allowing fresh air to blow in.

It seemed almost deserted on board, even though the car decks were quite full. Everyone had a bench or row of seats to lay across. As we exited the harbour we had a quick look out on deck and took some pictures of the sun rising before returning to the warmth of the café. It wasn’t long before I had my first coffee, with a very dry muffin. I ventured downstairs and watched a bit of a terrible looking film with Martin Lawrence and a pig in it, another café in full swing on the lounge floor had made the room smell a bit more bearable.

We looked over our plans for the first few days in the South Island again and made a few changes. We initially wanted to go to Kaikoura as it promised whale, dolphin and penguin sightings, but later decided against it as the only way to see them would be on expensive boat charters, or as the AA guide for the area recommended, from the air in a chartered helicopter! We knew that wasn’t going to happen, but on the ferry decided it would still be worth a look and wasn’t that far from our first planned destination, Blenheim.

Although it’s a four hour journey from Wellington to Picton, not much of it is spent in unprotected waters. Before long we entered the Marlborough Sounds, a maze of land rising from the sea, requiring every boat to navigate through it on the way to Picton Harbour. Hills rose steeply on both sides of the narrow stretch of water like huge walls. Their tops were hidden by a low lying fog which altogether gave you a kind of tunnel vision as you stared out at the water.

It was drizzling as we docked at Picton, and we hopped in the van and headed to Blenheim, centre of the best wine area New Zealand has to offer. We stopped at the I-site to get some information to go with what we had read in the Lonely Planet, and then made our way to Renwick, the next town along where Grove Mill Vineyard offered free tasting!

It was at this point that I realised I hadn’t actually changed out of the clothes I was sleeping in the night before, other than to throw a pair of jeans over my thermals for the early drive to the ferry. I changed in to something a bit more suitable in the vineyards car park before we headed inside, trying not to look like two people wanting to drink lots of free wine.

We were first poured a Sauvignon Blanc and directed towards six or so glasses on a table that each had a different addition to the glass. It was the same wine we had, but with cut grass, capsicum (red pepper), pineapple, passion fruit or kiwi fruit added. The idea was for you to smell each and see if you could then describe the Sauvignon Blanc better by understanding which smells if any came through. I was having a descriptive block and the only word that kept popping in to my head was ‘grapey’. I chose not to share it with the lady pouring our drinks.

Next we had a Riesling as it was one of Nat’s favourites, followed by a Pinot Noir (as recommended to us by Paul), a revered wine in the region that tasted, like its colour, much lighter than other reds I’d tried. Unfortunately it was the most expensive of the lot. We must have looked a bit stuck for a decision and the lady offered us a dessert wine, Gewürztraminer which the vineyard had only recently had the possibility of creating. We ended up buying two bottles as there was an offer on making the second only a third of the price.

We had some other vineyards written down that offered free tasting but based on a recommendation from Dr Pete (which sounded expensive) we headed to Wairau. They also happened to offer highly recommended meals, including a Cheese Soufflé and Steak Sandwich that immediately grabbed my attention. The lady offering the wine tasting was really friendly and we chatted about her friends in the UK and she gave us some tips on where to find tourist-free ski slopes further south. All the time we were trying various wines and in the end we decided rather than buying one, we should just have lunch there as it had been a while since we’d eaten out. The food was fantastic, living up to the hype and we left completely happy with our days exploits.

Having spent all that money on wine and good food (nearly fifty pounds!) we drove back through Blenheim and south in the direction of Kaikoura. In our trusty Conservation Campsites book we’d spotted a Department of Conservation campsite near Lake Grassmere overlooking Clifford Bay. It was a very basic site with no drinking water or power, but all we really needed were loos so it was a great chance to save some money on site fees. We drove eight kilometres off the main road down to the waters edge and found ourselves a pitch surrounded by trees backing on to the beach. We had a wander around the area and found a rope swing tied in a tree to mess about on, before returning to the van and cooking some food. It began to rain as we got ready for bed and once settled in for the night we could also here the noisy surf only ten or so metres away.

We didn’t really have a check-out time at the DoC site as it was unmanned and worked on you paying in an honesty box as you arrived. So we got up late (still raining) and had breakfast, before packing everything away ready for the drive to Kaikoura. We managed to move forward about a foot in the van before the rear wheels started to spin and we rolled backwards again. It was only a very slight incline, but overnight it had been completely soaked through and our van was struggling to find any grip. After a few more attempts, with me pushing at the back, and then two other campers trying to help as well, we were getting nowhere. A couple of trips to the beach and back later, with plenty of gravel under each rear wheel (thank god it wasn’t a soft sand beach) we managed to get the van back on to tarmac and onwards to Kaikoura.
