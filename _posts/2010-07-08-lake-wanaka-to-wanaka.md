date: 2010-07-08 00:28:00

The morning brought another lovely day and we were pleasently surprised to see that the slightly creepy campsite we arrived at last night had been transformed into a peaceful setting amongst trees and that we had camped just above Lake Wanaka which we had a great view of whilst eating our porridge.

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/07/11283428-P1040945.JPG)

After a quick look around Wanaka (it didn't take long) we headed for Lake Tekapo.

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/07/11283432-P1040949.JPG)

As we drove we started to notice more snow around and soon we were driving along roads with snow just at the side of us. This was quite surreal and we felt as if we had passed through the back of a certain magical wardrobe! The mountains around us were huge and covered with snow.

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/07/11283438-P1040957.JPG)

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/07/11283448-P1040958.JPG)

Arriving at Lake Tekapo we were greeted with an amazing view of a powder blue lake surrounded by snow covered mountains lit up by the afternoon sun. We went straight to the lake and took some pics, then checked out a statue of a Collie Dog put up to honor all the working dogs who helped the farmers in the area work the land and we also looked around a small stone Church overlooking the lake.

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/07/11283450-P1040959.JPG)

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/07/11283457-P1040968.JPG)

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/07/11283460-P1040974.JPG)

By this time the sun had dipped behind the mountains and the temperature instantly dropped so after a walk down the short parade of shops we checked in at the only campsite in the area and for this payed over what we had been expecting.

The next day we awoke feeling as if our trip to Lake Tekapo was without reason so after consulting the book we decided to walk up Mt John to get an arial view of the lake and mountains and also get a better look at Mt Cook which is the highest peak in NZ.

The walk was meant to take an hour to get up to the sumit which we almost managed but due to an extremely icy and in some places steep path it was a little bit more challanging than we thought.

Luckily there was a bench at the top so that we could recover and drink in the views which were well worth it.

Once at the top we realised that there was a road that came up the mountain too!

So after walking/sliding back down the Mt and feeling like we had achieved something in Tekapo we headed back along the road we arrived on to get to Queenstown.

Queenstown was buzzing when we arrived, people walking around dressed in their snow gear made us feel quite excited and strangley at home. We quickly parked up and entered into the busy streets, it was nice to be somewhere that had stuff going on.

The streets were packed with high end shops, bars and resturants, we ended up walking down to an area called Earnslaw Park on the lakefront and saw that there was a stage set up and a crew were putting together the final stages of a snow covered scaffolding structure containing a rail and an uphill box (technical boarding terms for stuff to jump off and do tricks!).

We knew that Queenstown was in the final few days of it's Winterfestival '10 and quickly found someone wearing a labelled jacket to get a programme of what was going on. We discovered that in fifty minutes a warm up and competition of the best skiers and boarders in the country was due to start and it was all free to watch! We raced back to the camper and got checked into a convienient campsite walking distance to town, put some more clothes on as it was chilling down again and got back down to the park.

It was really busy down there and a Dj was getting the atmosphere going, we watched the guys and girls setting off down the ramp and doing tricks off their chosen apparatus. Saw some sweet as tricks and some nasty looking crashes, once we had got too cold we went off to indulge and warm up with a mulled wine.

When we got back the final had just got going so we stuck around for a bit longer to watch the chosen ones doing backflips and 360's then as soon as the final was over we and everyone else scattered back to a warm place, ours was the good ole camper.

On the way back to the camper we had payed for another night which enabled us to have a bit of a lay in which was really nice. We got ourselves sorted and headed up to Coronet Peak which is one of Queenstown's ski slopes. Parked in the car park and got a ride up to the top on a shuttle bus driven by a guy who spent last summer lifeguarding at Bournemouth Beach, small world eh!

We walked out onto the busy mountain and felt instantly weird that we weren't wearing our boarding clothes, however our minds were soon turned to watching the activities going on to do with the Winterfestival.

There had already been a race down the mountain called the dash for cash where the winner got $500 and we were about to watch a race called grab a seat suitcase race, you can see where this one is going.

We watched some nutbars race downhill on all kinds of different suitcase, some had been modified so they had handles and others had just brought them as they were. One guy carrying his suitcase plus bodyboard and dressed in a wetsuit, mask, fins and a santa hat ran down the course, amazingly only tripping over his fins at the last minute and subsequently landing his face into another competitor. He was disqualified but did a rogue run by himself on his bodyboard inbetween heats.

This was all good fun to watch but as our toes had turned to ice we came back down the mountain to slightly warmer temps. The rest of the day was spent stocking up on food and chilling out in the van. We went back into town for a wander and found ourselves in the AJ Hacket shop watching bungy and swing videos trying to decide what to choose. Luckily we have a few days before we are back in Queenstown so will have made a choice by then. Grabbed a happy hour mulled wine before getting back to the van for the night.

For our final morning in Queenstown we decided to walk up to Bob's peak which is a great lookout over the city, we also wanted to look at the ledge bungy and swing site. There is a gondola which takes you up but it's a bit pricey and the walk was only supposed to take an hour, however it ended up being more of a hike and I vowed that this was the last walk I would be doing in NZ! The views were fab and we saw the bungy ledge, also saw people tandem paragliding which has sewn another seed of an extreme thing to do.

We practically ran back down the hill taking some bike tracks as short cuts to get back to the car before we got a ticket, we made it and drove out of Queenstown.

We were heading back to Wanaka to have five days of snowboarding and on the way stopped off at the Kawarau Bridge to watch some people jumping off the worlds first commercial bungy site. This was pretty cool and we chatted to a few people there, some who had done it others who were just observing and one girl who had gone out to do it but just couldn't bring herself to make the final leap (she didn't get a refund!).

Back at the van we grabbed some lunch before carrying on to Wanaka.

It was nice to arrive back at somewhere we already knew and made our way to the chosen campsite which was to be our home for the next six nights. Laura who checked us in gave us 10% discount for staying more than two nights and also free internet for our stay, sweet!

The campsite was really nice and you could tell the owners had put a lot of effort into making it a fab place to stay, especially for skiers and boarders.

One of the reasons we chose the site was that it had a drying room for snow clothes and a ski work room for waxing and tuning boards and ski's. Not to mention the sauna, hot tubs, free showers and under floor heating in the bathrooms!! We still had to sleep in the van but these little extras would make the stay a bit nicer.

We had a little look around, met some of the other campers and then made use of the hot tub before getting an early night ready for our first day on the slopes.
