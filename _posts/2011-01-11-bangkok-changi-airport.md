The journey to Bangkok was uneventful. The heavily decorated coach had TV’s on board that past the first few hours showing the film Superman Returns. About an hour from the Banglamphu area we were staying in, we started to notice the city appearing around us. The roads grew wider and buildings jumped from single-storeys dwellings in to multi-storey tower-blocks. We had decided - as touristy as it was - that Thao Kao San (also known as Kao San Road) would be the best location for our brief encounter with Bangkok. It was close enough to the historic and holy sights we wanted to see and accommodation was within our budget.

We walked the back streets from the bus drop-off point to the guesthouse we had chosen, managing to avoid the main road hassle of cars, bikes, people and hundreds of locals all working on small commissions to fill the many hotels and guesthouses. We got the last cheap room then made our way to the chaos of Kao San Rd, just out of earshot of our bed.

The neon bright rows of shops, bars, hawkers and food stalls were loud but not aggressive. The only really crazy thing to me was that bikes, cars, taxis and minibuses frequently tried to drive down the road which was crammed full of people. Having eaten before and during our bus journey, we just grabbed a few snacks from the food carts - spring rolls and beef skewers - before stopping off in a third-floor bar to listen to a guitarist play solo and have two overpriced beers. We justified our choice with the great people watching vantage point looking down on the road below, and nursed the drinks until one waitress actually checked my bottle to see if I had any left. We finished the evening with a Thai massage, Nat opting for a head, neck and shoulder, while I went for a foot massage after spending most of the day walking the streets.

The next morning we went for breakfast at a really nice restaurant at the opposite end of Kao San Rd before getting a tuk-tuk to the Temple of the Emerald Buddha. We decided beforehand not to actually go in to the temple as it was too expensive, but we walked around the outside admiring it from afar, on the way to Wat Pho, to see the reclining Buddha for a much more reasonable entrance fee.

Wat Pho itself is the oldest and largest temple in Bangkok, dating from the 16th century. It houses the country’s largest reclining Buddha; and it has the biggest collection of Buddha images in Thailand. The star of the show, the reclining Buddha is forty-six metres long and fifteen metres high, finished in gold leaf. There was a huge crowd squeezing through the entranceway and passing along the hall taking pictures of the enlightened one. After walking the gauntlet once, Nat decided she wanted to take more pictures and headed back in for a second look. It was worth it as she got some great shots.

We wanted to see some of the other parts of Bangkok like Siam Square and Sukhumvit, but the distances between areas were too large to walk and the traffic was so bad that most of the time in a tuk-tuk was spent stationary in a fog of exhaust fumes. The Banglamphu area is a few kilometres west of the Skytrain service linking the more affluent parts of Bangkok, so we decided to see what we could on foot and leave it at that.

That evening we walked the market streets again and stopped off outside the guesthouse Mark & Sam stayed in to try the Pad Thai at one of the food stalls. With our hunger satisfied we went back to one stall to continue negotiations on a bag Nat really wanted, and eventually got it for a good price.

Our final day in Bangkok was very relaxed; we got up not long before check-out and walked back to yesterdays Pad Thai stall for a cheap breakfast. We only had two hours before our minibus arrived to take us to the airport, so we had a last look through the stalls before loading our bags and heading east to Suvarnabhumi International Airport. The traffic was surprisingly light and we arrived with plenty of time, only then to find our flight was also delayed. We checked in and with very little Thai Baht left, we hunted for the best value food. It seemed criminal that a poor excuse for Pad Thai in the airport was ten times the price of the same sized dish offered at a street stall. We ended up eating sandwiches from a coffee shop and snacking on the free samples offered at another counter. Our gate was finally open and we were soon aboard and heading to Singapore; for the third time on this trip!

We landed at Changi Airport and walked straight from arrivals to departures. As we were flying with Singapore Airlines this time (part of our round-the-world ticket we bought long ago) we were able to check-in up to two days before our planned departure. We were staying at the airport overnight, saving us a night’s accommodation in pricey Singapore. Due to our late flight from Bangkok, we would only actually be in the airport for eight hours, and Changi is arguably one of the nicest airports to kill time in.

We still had some Singapore currency left from our previous hops through the Asian transport hub, so we planned to spend that and no more whilst waiting for our next flight. We started with McDonald’s. Cheap fast food was just what was required after surviving on free samples of miniature rice crackers in Suvarnabhumi Airport. Terminal 2 at Changi has a few gardens, including a Sunflower garden outside on one section of roof overlooking the flight gates. It has an entertainment area with a cinema room, gaming computers and a sound studio where you can play Guitar Hero. We were happy with the free internet and lounging areas with big comfy bean bags. Just before settling down for the night, we shared a huge mug of Starbucks hot chocolate.

Our flight was departing on time and at the gate we were moved to exit row seats for a bit more leg room. It soon became clear why the last minute change was so easy; as the doors were locked for take-off the plane could only have been half full. The in-flight menu had some Indian choices, so we sipped masala tea and I had a chicken curry; the last non-vegetarian meal I had planned until we were safely on British soil.

India would be a delicious assault on the taste-buds and I wasn’t in the slightest concerned that for the next four weeks, I would be going without cooked animal.
