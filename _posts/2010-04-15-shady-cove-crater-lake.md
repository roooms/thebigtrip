---
title: "Shady Cove & Crater Lake"
date: 2010-04-15 00:14:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
From Portland we headed back west to continue driving the scenic Route 101. We stopped briefly at Depoe Bay and the amazing sand dunes along the Oregon coast, before heading back inland toward our destination, Shady Cove.

Driving in to the centre of Shady Cove we were greeted with single storey wooden buildings facing on to the main highway, a two-way street that ran through and out the other side of the 2 mile square city.

At first we were worried there may be no restaurants of cafes to eat at, but as we ventured further looking for our motel for the night, we passed a Subway and knew that we could at least get a sandwich if all else failed.

We parked up at the Royal Coachman Motel and went to the front office to collect our room key. There we met the owner Jerry, who kindly reduced the room rate because we had travelled so far. He also recommended the sports bar opposite for a good burger and beer. Just what we needed.

Walking in to the bar was just like any traveller expects in a small quiet 'city', everyone stared at us (all 3 of them). The owner, bartender and sole customer all welcomed us and we got to chatting about where we were from and why we were here. 'Bo' the only other person sat our side of the bar had lived his whole life in Shady Cove, but travelled around the surrounding states a lot as a truck driver. He now ran a taxi/tour guide service in the area, and listed a handful of great places we should see tomorrow if we had time.

Throughout the evening a few more people arrived, most notably 'Ronnie Two Guns', who was also very knowledgeable, but considerably more drunk. We played some pool, and left after a few more friendly, but slightly scary, 'paraletic' ex-cons joined Ronnie in making us the centre of attention.

The next morning we checked out and ventured next door for what turned out to be a great breakfast, again meeting the owner, and this time his whole family, before driving further along highway 62, closing in on our target of Crater Lake.

On the way we stopped off at Natural Bridge, as recommended by Bo the previous night, then wound our way up from 1,400 to around 6,200 feet above sea level to the rim of Crater Lake. We had to enter Crater Lake from the west as it is the only access road maintained during the winter. We had no idea what to expect but the pictures show the snow does get a bit deep.

Once at the rim, we parked the car and walked to the edge of the crater. The view was just astounding. Six miles long and five miles wide, the almost circular pristine blue lake was only disturbed by a rock formation known as Wizard Island. Once we'd had our fill of the beautiful views, we headed back down the highway, passing Shady Cove on the way to Crescent City, California.
