We had an early start and after grabbing a Dairylea baguette (which is becoming a staple breakfast in Laos) and some water we were on our minibus and off to the 4000 Islands.

At the small port town we boarded a long tail boat which took us over to one of the Islands, Don Khon This was our first stop and after carefully getting our bags off without dropping them in the water we went off to find ourselves a reasonable room.

We ended up in a bungalow not far from the water, we went out for a wander and ended up down by the bridge that connects Don Khon to Don Det. We had some food there and then wandered back to chill out for the evening.

The next day was our day of adventure, we hired bikes and rode around the whole Island. We found a little bar at one end of the island to have a beer in which it clearly suggested was a good idea. Next we saw a waterfall and played with a little puppy before carrying on through rice paddies dotted with water buffalo.

In the afternoon we rode back to the south point of the island to go on a dolphin watching trip. The Irrawaddy dolphin is really rare but is commonly sighted in the morning and afternoon so we found our boatman and headed out. We had to go onto Cambodian soil to pay them a small fee to use their waters as well as Laos which was cool as we weren’t supposed to be there without a visa!

Saw loads of Dolphins during the hour we were out on the water and it was really calm out there.

After coming back to shore we rode back to our bungalow and had a little rest after our busy day before going out to find some late food.

We decided to head over to Don Det the next day as it was meant to be a bit busier. We took a boat over to the island and found an ok bungalow painted baby pink. After a rest and trying out the square mozzy net we went out for a walk around the island. We saw the other side of the bridge, more rice paddies and some huge water buffalo. This island had a few more western eating options which were just ok and a couple of bars but we didn’t think the place was much busier.

We hadn’t really thought about how long it would take to walk around the island and we ended up getting back in the dark and really hungry so went straight to a restaurant. The place we went to was not a great success and we decided to call it a night and to also leave the next day and go to Phnom Penh, Cambodia for a bit of city life.
