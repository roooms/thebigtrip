---
title: "Banda Aceh"
date: 2010-08-26 05:27:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
It was our first time paying for a visa on our trip. We went to the visa application desk and found it was cash only, so we had to go to an ATM to withdraw some Indonesian Rupiah. A twenty-five day visa cost 230,000 rupiah or about £16. This would also be the first country to give our brains a proper maths workout.

Whilst all this was going on a porter had already collected our bags and was looking forward to seeing how generous our tipping would be. The taxi fare from the airport to the centre of Banda Aceh gave us our first experience of how the locals drive. It looked like gridlock, but everything was moving at pace and small motorbikes would come from nowhere to fill the almost non-existent gaps between trucks and cars. Nobody had right of way at junctions, everyone just beeped their horn and pulled out assuming that was enough warning of their intentions.

Arriving at Hotel Prapat we paid for two nights and made our way up to our room on the second floor. We had opted for a room with a fan as air-conditioning cost double, but we did get a ‘western’ style toilet and 2 litres of bottled water each day included in the bargain price. We dropped our bags and went out to find a supermarket I had read about where we could get a plug-in mosquito deterrent.

We were clearly in a minority of tourists in Banda Aceh and everyone waved, said hello and asked us where we came from. While waiting to cross a busy road, even a passing army truck full of armed guards waved and shouted greetings to us. It re-affirmed everything we had read about the area being a welcoming place to visit.

Back at the hotel one of the few taxi drivers waiting at the gate introduced himself in very reasonable English. His name was Adlias and he was also an English teacher at a local school. During Ramadan school was closed so he worked all day driving his taxi. He gave us a run down of the sights to see, most of which had some relation to the devastating tsunami that flattened Banda Aceh on Boxing Day in 2004. There was enough time before dusk (when Adlias would be rushing off to get some food and drink) to get out to the ‘boat in the house’ in Lampulo.

On our return the night market was just starting to come alive, so we changed in to clothing more appropriate for the mosquito onslaught and covered ourselves in repellent before walking over to the market for some food. The food was great and the market full of locals eating having spent the daylight hours fasting for Ramadan.

The next morning we took a ride with Adlias to some of the other sites in town. Another popular attraction following the tsunami was a 2,600 ton ship/floating power generator that had been swept 6km inland from its anchoring point and sits in the middle of a residential area. From there we went to the ‘long beach’ and checked out how warm the water was, and then made our way over to a rocky shoreline where kids were fishing and the new concrete plant that was tasked with providing the materials to rebuild Banda Aceh had been constructed.

That evening we grabbed some supplies from the supermarket to keep us going the following morning before our ferry to Pulau Weh.

Back at the hotel room I tried to call my dad to wish him Happy Birthday. After fifteen minutes of changing various settings I concluded the phone wasn’t going to work in Indonesia. I went down to reception to see if their phone could call internationally, it couldn’t; so I started the walk to the main street and its many internet cafes to send my sister a message to pass on over the phone. On the way I stopped at a travel agency that was still open and checked if they had a phone that would work. No luck there, but one of the customers suggested I buy a local SIM card to make the call. He (Jamil) offered to take me to purchase one from a shop, and soon after I found myself riding pillion on the back of his Honda Tiger. It was a strange situation riding around town at night looking for a mobile phone shop to make a single call, but it was worthwhile. In the end with no luck getting a SIM, we topped up his mobile and he let me use it to call home. It was great to be able to speak to my dad to wish him a Happy Birthday, and it was all thanks to Jamil. It reminded me of a book I read early on in our trip, called The Kindness of Strangers.

Following a bad nights sleep in the sticky heat we were up early for our journey to Uleh-leh, Banda Aceh’s port, and a ferry across to the island of Pulau Weh. We planned to do some more diving there, reportedly the best spot in the Indian Ocean.
