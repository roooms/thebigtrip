We arrived in a very rainy Kuala Lumpur after a pretty relaxing train journey. It took about seven and a half hours which meant the film Ned Kelly was shown about two and a half times broken up by random outbursts of Asian techno music, but apart from that it was relaxing!

We left Mum and Dad at the ‘KL Sentral Station’ to get a cab to their hotel whilst we got the subway train to the Little India area. (I can see a pattern forming here!) We walked around in the rain for a while as we tried to work out where the hotel was. Finally after realising we had gone in a circle we asked some construction workers and a girl working in the tube station and were pointed in the right direction.

After dumping our stuff and checking out our room we went back out into the rain to find some food, everything was shut, even Mcdonalds, so we settled for some Ritz crackers, strange pastry’s filled with green sweet stuff (Kaya) and a couple of bananas.

The next morning we didn’t wake up till nearly eleven as we hadn’t set an alarm and our room had no windows! Still it was nice to have some extra sleep.

We met Mum and Dad in the market outside our hotel as they had moved to ours for the next couple of days.

We all took a walk to Central Market which is nice as it is just like an outdoor market but it’s air conditioned. We were definitely feeling the heat more than Singapore so found ourselves ducking into buildings more frequently. We got some food in the market and then split up for the afternoon.

Me and Dan took a walk through Chinatown which was bigger and more gaudy than the one at Singapore which seemed cute in comparison. We carried onto Merdeka Square which is a nice grass area in the centre of KL and used to be a cricket pitch, we had a rest here and worked out where to go next.

The colonial district was nearby so we walked through that and came to Masjid Negara which is one of the biggest Mosque’s in SE Asia. We decided to go and have a look around but first we had to get gowned up!

After a walk around the very ornate Mosque we tried to find the Lake Gardens. This turned out to be more difficult than we anticipated; we could see the gardens and the lake but could not find a way into them! In the end we decided we would be too tired to actually walk around them if we found a way in so started to walk back to town. On the way we passed the bird park and saw a cheeky Hornbill bird tapping on the window of a restaurant for food.

Later on we met Mum and Dad and went to a Chinese place on the street for some food. Deciding against the chicken feet or frogs legs we had some tasty noodles, spring rolls, calamari and some sweet coconut buns. Washed down with a few big bottles of Tiger beer it all came to the grand total of about £27, bargin!

Our next day was a fairly early start to get over to the Petronas Towers in a hope to get some free tickets to go up onto the Skybridge. Each day from 8.30am they allocate 1600 tickets on a first come first serve basis. By the time we got there at 9.40am all the tickets were already gone!

We decided to get up earlier and try again the next day so headed off to met Mum and Dad at the bus stop to go to the Batu Caves.

It was chaos at the bus station, with no timetable we waited for our bus to arrive. Just as I was saying to Mum about getting a cab instead bus U6 turned up with a very happy driver who got off shouting “Batucay..Batucay..Batucay..Batucay” we had worked out he was saying Batu Caves but an elderly Indian man translated it for us too.

The bus journey was good once we got out of the gridlocked traffic of KL centre and we got to see some different parts of the city, arriving at Batu Caves we were stunned by a huge golden statue standing outside an equally big cave next to a rather large amount of steps.

The Batu Caves are a system of three caves, the most famous is Temple Cave as it contains a Hindu Shrine. We walked up the 272 steps to reach the temple which is guarded by the 43m high Murga statue, the highest in the world. It is also guarded but quite a few monkeys which I expect can cause mayhem if they want to but were quite well behaved while we were there. The caves were huge and dotted with different Hindu statues and monkeys!

After a snack we took a bus back to town and Me and Dan treated our weary feet to a fish foot massage. This was definitely one of the strangest experiences I have ever had and even though my feet are not as ticklish as Dan’s it was a struggle to keep them in the water!

You basically sit with your feet in a large pool of water and the fish clean any dead skin off your feet and legs, this doesn’t hurt as they don’t have teeth but you can definitely feel them nibbling your feet! The first two minutes were the worst (although Dan would say the first eight minutes were) then once you relax it becomes quite therapeutic. After ten minutes and having inspected my lovely smooth feet I was talking about getting it done again the next day.

That evening we went out for a veggie Indian meal at a place where the food was excellent but the service shocking!

On our final full day we got up even earlier and raced over to the towers, we arrived at 8.38am and there was a whole foyer full of people. I couldn’t believe it, they were saying all the tickets were gone but let a few of us line up in a separate queue to see if there were any left over. After about half an hour of waiting we decided that it was looking like a no go so we went to get some breakfast which consisted of a strange but good sweetbread roll filled with cheese and topped with chocolate drops.

We went back to the hotel to make some bookings for the next week of travel and check out of our hotel, we had to stay another night but the one we were in had no rooms free. We met Mum and Dad in reception and said goodbye to them, they were heading off on another train journey north to Panang.

It had been so nice to spend some time with Mum and Dad and have some travelling buddies that the rest of the day was a bit strange. We checked into our new hotel and were glad it was just for one night, caught up on the blog and wrote some postcards. Later on we had a craving for western food so ended up at Mcdonalds, bad I know but it was the first one we have had on the whole trip, well apart from my late night happy meal munch in Sydney after a few drinks!

Our journey to the airport the next day was pretty chaotic, first check out took a little while longer than expected then a guy in the lift shunned our plans of getting to the airport and advised us to save money and get the airport bus. We had to get money out and by the time we got to the bus stop I think we had just missed a bus and the next one was in half an hours time, the guy we were talking to said it would take too long and we should get a cab. The taxi driver wanted 75 ringgit but we only had 58 which he took and we bundled into the cab. Getting out of town was not too bad but the journey to the airport seemed to take forever and in the end we had to ask him to put his foot down or we were going to miss our flight!

Finally we arrived and raced into the airport, luckily we had checked in online so only had to bag drop which we did with a guy who kindly let us use his excess baggage desk, next we were through customs and security and off to the gate with a quick stop to buy food and water as we needed to take our Malaria tablets. There was no-one at our gate when we arrived but a guy from another gate came over looked at our boarding passes and told us where to go, we ran outside to the plane and got there as the last of the on time passengers were boarding…phew!

We settled into our seats to enjoy the one hour twenty minute flight to Banda Aceh which would be a much quicker flight but quite a different destination than our first one, New York, exactly six months ago!
