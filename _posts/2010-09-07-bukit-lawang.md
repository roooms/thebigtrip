title: "Bukit Lawang"
date: 2010-09-07 07:10:00
author: "Natalie"
---
During our bus journey to Bukit Lawang we had a few locals introduce themselves to us and advertise their services. But one guy, Selamat was a cool guy with great English and a registered guide card so we decided to do a jungle trek with him the following day. He ended up showing us to our hotel which was an interesting journey across fields, next to a river where we saw kids playing on a swing over the river, and through the village of Bukit Lawang which is right in the jungle and next to the river.

We finally arrived at Green Hill, dumped our bags in our nice room and headed back out with Selamat.

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/09/14731397-P1050934.JPG)

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/09/14732115-P1060138.JPG)

Everyone in the village seemed friendly and we got a free and very well used map from tourist info. Then we went to a place called Tony’s pizza for Dan’s birthday pizza. It wasn’t too bad, a nice change from the local food we have been eating but I think we both missed the spice!

We negotiated what length of trek we wanted to do and we decided to go for a one day trek to give ourselves the best chance of seeing Orang-utan in the wild. We paid up hoping that he would turn up tomorrow and went back to our digs in the rain.

Green Hill is run by an English girl called Andrea and her Sumatran husband, she is a conservationist working towards the wellbeing of the Orang-utan in the area. The guest house is mostly made of wood and our room was nice, natural and basic with a mattress on the floor covered by a mosquito net but with a great view of the jungle across the water. We got freshened up which was a struggle as it meant pouring cold water from a bucket over ourselves but we managed and went to chill in the lounging area for the evening.

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/09/14731418-P1050956.JPG)

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/09/14731450-P1050957.JPG)

We were up bright and early and met Selamat who was a bit late but thankfully turned up! Then we were off into the jungle. Selamat was really knowledgeable and showed us rubber trees being bled, we tasted clove leaves and smelt lemongrass.

We went a bit further and saw a group of Long-tailed Macaques otherwise known as cheeky monkeys and then some Giant Ants and a strange looking moth.

Our first sighting of an Orang-utan wasn’t what I thought it would be, we came across a lone adult quite high in a tree, surrounded by about 15 people and he was holding a big bottle of energy drink which he had either taken or had been given to draw him closer. I’m not sure which but it wasn’t a good sight and he looked pretty unhappy. Before our trek we had read some rules and been told by Andrea that it is stressful for the apes to have large crowds of people around them and they should not be closer to you than 10 metres so knowing this we told Selamat we wanted to move on. Tempting as it was to stay we decided we wanted to see them looking a bit more natural.

We stopped for a fruit break by a little stream and then carried on back up a steep track, it wasn’t long before we came across our first proper sighting of four apes in the trees. It was really exciting and a bit scary as they are wild and very strong but they stayed in the trees eating and swinging around occasionally checking us out.

We didn’t stay there too long as other people were turning up and so carried on a bit until we came to a Thomas Leaf monkey sitting on a tree stump, he was pretty chilled as we checked him out but then something made him move on. We turned down the path and saw some cheeky monkeys and a very large Orang-utan called Suma coming towards us on the ground and she had her baby attached to her. This was a great sight and we kept our distance as we watched her coming along the path, she kept her eyes on us as she made her way then finally turned down a different direction. We all breathed a sigh of relief and wonder as we turned away and went back down to a stream for lunch.

Selamat had brought a feast with him, as well as the fruit he had a nasi goreng (fried rice with veg) for each of us which included the egg, cucumber and tomato plus prawn crackers. Then he cut up a pineapple, we were stuffed and had to rest for a while before we could face walking on our extremely full stomach’s. We saw a couple of turtles in the stream who turned up when Selamat threw a bit of rice in, they obviously get used to him turning up for lunch!

We carried on walking for a bit longer but that was our lot for the day, not that we were disappointed as we had seen what we came for.

Once we came out of the jungle we went to a bat cave which was good but we were both pretty shattered after our 8 hour trek to really take it in so we headed back to our home and relaxed with a pumpkin curry while listening to the huge thunderstorm overhead and the guys from the lodge playing guitar.

The next day was really lazy, we stayed at the lodge almost all day reading our books and watching the jungle and river. Selamat turned up for us to go tubing with him down the river which we were really up for but what we had thought was going to be a friendly thing turned into a business opportunity and he wanted lots of money from us. We declined and in the end just enjoyed the peace of the balcony for the rest of the day.

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/09/14732096-P1060115.JPG)

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/09/14732079-P1060104.JPG)

The next morning we were up and out by about 11, we walked halfway to the bus station and then got a becak the rest of the way. We managed to get a minibus back to Medan for the same price we paid on the way to Bukit Lawang but it was a much more uncomfortable journey and only marginally quicker!

Once we arrived we took another becak to the airport where we finally found the domestic terminal and checked in for our flight to Jakarta in Java.

I was a little bit nervous about our flight on Sriwijaya Air especially as over half the seats were empty but it was all fine and we arrived in Jakarta a couple of hours later.

We took a very comfy people carrier (the only way into the city in the evening) into Jakarta and to a backpacker street called Jalan Jaksa where we found an ok room and went out for a little wander then crashed after a full on day of travel.
