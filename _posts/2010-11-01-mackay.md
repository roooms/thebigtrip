title: "Mackay"
date: 2010-11-01 02:03:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
Mackay was a bit of an unusual place for a stop on the east coast, but we had worked out that we couldn’t afford a trip around the Whitsunday’s and therefore it wasn’t worth stopping at Airlie Beach. Mackay would be a stopover and we would leave the next day for Agnes Water and Town of 1770.

We arrived at 8pm and headed for a nice backpackers called Gecko’s Rest. It only had one room left which we snapped up before going for a walk around the busy end of town. The sun had not long-ago set and there were hundreds of noisy birds returning to their nests amongst the trees. The busy part of Mackay is about three blocks by two and consists mainly of hotels, bars and take-away food shops. According to The Lonely Planet guide there was also a park and botanical gardens that we would check out the next day.

The following morning we checked out of our hostel but were able to leave our bags locked up safely until 9pm so we could take a look around Mackay during daylight. We would be leaving Mackay just before 2am that night so had a long day ahead of us. We walked towards the Pioneer River and found a new esplanade that wound its way west towards a new lagoon that had been built at the rivers edge. It was free to enter but as we had no idea it existed, we hadn’t come prepared. We made our way south; back in to the centre of Mackay to see a central park and art museum, then east toward the orchid and botanical gardens. It was a mammoth walk although we didn’t actually find the botanical gardens in the end.

We made it back to Gecko’s Rest and the great staff offered us the use of the sun deck until they locked up. Nat caught up with the blog and I caught up with some… sleep, before we grabbed our bags and started the walk to… McDonalds! The only place open 24 hours in Mackay and offering free Wi-Fi. We spent 4 hours (yes 4 hours!) at McDonalds, drinking strawberry milkshakes and coffee and people watching. As we walked along the main street to the Greyhound terminal we were surrounded by club leavers who were drunkenly trying to organise food or a lift home, then we loaded our bags aboard our night bus and tried to find a seat amongst all the backpackers who had boarded at Airlie Beach a few hours before. We scored a seat at the back and tried to get comfortable for the long journey ahead but less than an hour later we pulled in to a roadhouse where we had a thirty minute break to get some refreshments while the coach was refuelled. Oh well. Back on the bus we had a four hour stretch before our next quick break in Rockhampton, then the final stint to Agnes Water where our hostel had arranged for a shuttle bus to collect us.
