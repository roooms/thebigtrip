The plane that would take us from Rarotonga to Aitutaki was a 15 seat, twin propeller with an open cockpit so you could see the pilots fiddling with switches and levers as we accelerated down the runway for take-off. The flight was uneventful until we were approaching Aitutaki and flew in to thick rain clouds. Safely on the ground we had to run from the plane to the terminal in the downpour, and await the truck that would bring our hold luggage to us.

There were no security checks at either end of the flight and we were greeted by our transfer driver Lexie, who had a ‘Lei’ for each of us. We didn’t get any on arrival in Rarotonga, maybe a sign of its size now. We jumped in her van and headed off to our accommodation at ‘Samade on the Beach’.

It was about a five minute drive from the airport and we waited in the reception for the rain to ease before heading to our bungalow. Aitutaki had been hit hard by a cyclone in February and some of the bungalows had tiles missing from their roofs and the surrounding coconut palms were looking a bit ragged. Our home for the next seven nights was nice though, on stilts with a veranda with table and chairs at the front, sliding doors leading to the studio style bed/living/kitchen area and a separate bathroom.

Birds, chickens and even piglets could be found rooting around in the soil under the bungalows. There only seemed to be one rooster in the area as well, which would hopefully make for quieter mornings as two or three always seemed to compete to be the noisiest.

The rain soon passed and we made our way back towards reception and the bar/restaurant on the beach right next to the lagoon. The water was turquoise with shoals of silver fish with yellow or black tails milling around at the surface. We walked along the beach out to where the lagoon ended and we could see the rough sea crashing against its outer edge.

Back at the bar we grabbed a couple of beers and put on them on our room tab, before heading back to the bungalow for a shower. We ventured back out again for some food at the restaurant. It was similar in price to eating out in the UK, but that was something we couldn’t afford every night. We had brought food from Rarotonga as we had been warned shopping here could be expensive due to it all being air-freighted across and the cyclone causing crop damage.

The following day we just had time to eat breakfast before we were picked up early for our lagoon cruise. It was booked as part of our ‘island hopper’ package and gave us a free nights accommodation at Samade, which made the cruise almost free. It was an all day trip around the outer islands of Aitutaki’s lagoon and included lunch.

The weather over the previous couple of days hadn’t been great and now it was perfect, so three boats would be going out with around twenty five people all wanting to snorkel. We stopped off at three different sites to snorkel and saw hundreds of fish, green and purple local clams, and giant Australian clams that were introduced to the area and were now causing problems for the indigenous species. I filmed a bit with our new underwater camera we picked up in LA and hope to get some video on here at some point.

On our boat were four other couples, one from Scotland, one Australia, one New Zealand and one from Switzerland. We got chatting to the Swiss couple, Kristin and Gian-Marco who were on a six month trip travelling east and had some great tips on where to dive and snorkel in Indonesia.

Our main tour guide, a very big character called Te King assisted Channel 4 when they came to Aitutaki to film Shipwrecked a few years ago, and we stopped at Shark Island on our way around the inside edge of the lagoon.

Our second to last stop was on ‘One Foot Island’ where we had our passports stamped on arrival and were allowed free roam for thirty minutes. There was one building on the island and it took around twenty minutes to walk the beach all the way round, so we got to see pretty much all of it in the time we were there.

We had run out of time to disembark on the last island of the tour, but stopped just offshore of it and our captain explained that we were actually floating on Aitutaki’s original runway. Seaplane was the only way to get to Aitutaki before the concrete version was built on the mainland.

The tour ended on the east coast of Aitutaki (we had set off from the west) and Te King drove us back to our bungalow through his village, Tautu. Kristin and Gian-Marco invited us to join them for dinner that evening, and we accepted Te King’s invitation to church on the coming Sunday.

Over dinner we discussed where we had been and were going next. Kristin and Gian-Marco had six weeks left of their trip and were heading in to South America to meet family. They lived in Zermatt and we exchanged contact details along with the offer of accommodation should we ever go snowboarding in Switzerland again.

Friday and Saturday were spent lounging by the lagoon reading books and occasionally going for a swim when it got too hot to handle. The weather was a lot better than we’d had in Rarotonga, so Nat was in tanning mode and I was racing through Bill Bryson’s book ‘Down Under’, a record of his visits to Australia. Laying on the beach on Saturday afternoon, we were very surprised to see a sow and her piglets being lead down to the lagoon for a cooling dip in the water.

After a few days of eating corned beef and processed cheese slice sandwiches, we booked a meal at Café Tupuna on the Saturday night, a restaurant that was widely regarded as the best on the island. It was also one in only two or three that were open in the evening so it made choosing quite easy. Nat had Wahoo fish in a light batter with pumpkin and I had Tuna steak with risotto. We finished it off with a shared homemade cheesecake. The food was fantastic and again couldn’t be considered expensive for a holiday, but not something that could be repeated frequently on our budget.

Sunday we were picked up before ten by Te King and taken along with some other guests that had been on his cruise to his church in Tautu. It was very different to our experience on Rarotonga, much more traditionally Christian. The church was grand and beautifully decorated, and most of the service was in Maori with occasional translations in to English for the guests.

Afterwards Te King took us to one of the highest points on the island so we could look out over all of Aitutaki and he explained how and where the cyclone hit and what it damaged most. After being dropped back at the bungalow we grabbed one of the free kayaks and took it for a paddle around the coast near our beach. The on-site restaurant was doing an all-day BBQ which was good value so we had our evening meal there before heading back to the room to catch up on diary writing and photo sorting.

On Monday we walked to Paporoa rentals, about five minutes from Samade and hired a scooter to see as much of the island as possible in one day. We were also going to pick up supplies for our trip to Atiu which we were warned was even more remote and expensive than Aitutaki, and post some postcards home. We had lunch at Koru Café and spent some time using the painfully slow internet connection. Then it was back to the beach for a beer and seat watching the sunset.

Tuesday was our last full day on Aitutaki and as it was another sunny day we slapped on the sun cream and lounged by the lagoon. We took a kayak out later in the day once the tide had come in, and paddled around and passed the very pricey ‘Aitutaki Lagoon Resort & Spa’ where some people were paying ten times as much as us to be on the opposite side of the lagoon on their own private motu.

Tuesday night was ‘Island Night’ at Samade but we decided to skip the buffet as it was a bit over budget. The show was free for Samade guests though, so we made it to the bar around 8pm for a drink before it started. The music was live this time with about ten people playing instruments and there were two different groups of dancers. It was a little less choreographed but great to see some kids involved and enjoying themselves. At the end of the night, as expected, guests were dragged up to the dance floor to join in. I managed to escape but Nat was one of the first picked.

Wednesday morning consisted of repacking everything we had enjoyed spreading around the bungalow over the past seven days and laying by the lagoon until it was time to catch our flight to Atiu!
