---
title: "Arcata, Eureka and the Samoa Peninsula"
date: 2010-04-16 22:59:00
author: "Natalie"
---
Leaving Crescent City, the weather wasn't great but the scenery was still fantastic. We passed through more Redwood forests and drove along more amazing coastlines before arriving at Patrick's Point State Park. We stopped for lunch and explored the fairly small parks surroundings

Walked down to Agate Beach and wandered along for a while then climbed up Wedding Rock, apparently one of the parks most romantic spots, don't worry, nothing happened!

Saw some wildlife then carried on to Arcata.

Found ourselves another bargin nights accommodation and celebrated at The Humboldt Brewing Co, a bar/restaurant found in our trusty California Coast Lonely Planet book! As it turned out we ordered what had been recommended and it was fab, nice work LP!

The next morning had a proper wander through Arcata, it is really nice, laid back. Full of hippies and I swear you can smell green every corner you turn!

We got ourselves an ice-cream and sat in the center of the town just watching Arcata go by it's daily business.

Next we went 8 miles down the road to Eureka, quite a bit bigger than Arcata, more Victorian style buildings and apparently more straight laced. We had a wander around the buildings and checked out all the murals.

Then we found the fairly new Boardwalk after speaking to mum, very cute and we even saw Seals bobbing around the harbor!

By now we were feeling a bit peckish so headed off on a tip-off from one of the guys we met in Shady Cove to find a place called the Samoa Cookhouse. It was not far from Eureka and based on a peninsula of beaches and sand dunes which we had a look at first.

Arriving at the cookhouse we walked in to a completely empty room of tables all neatly set up ready to serve.

I think the main dining room could have seated about 100 people and then there were a couple of extra ones off to the sides of the main one.

We were greeted and seated by very friendly staff and then had the concept of the cookhouse explained to us. The menu was a set one but we could have as much of anything on it as we wanted!

We had a lovely, big late lunch which consisted of Vegetable Soup, Fresh bread, Salad, Pot Roast and fresh veg, Carrot Cake, Coffee and Tea and Water. All the food was homemade and you could taste it.

Needless to say we were stuffed and all that for about £8 each, you can't go wrong!

We looked around the place afterwards as it had a little museum attached as it used to be the place that all the Log-men working at nearby forests would eat at and this is how they would eat. At long tables with fresh food and as much as they wanted. The motto was that they never wanted a Log-man to leave saying he could have eaten more.

We definitely couldn't have eaten another thing so got back into the car and headed to our destination for the night, Ferndale.
