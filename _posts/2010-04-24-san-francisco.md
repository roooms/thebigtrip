---
title: "San Francisco"
date: 2010-04-24 09:58:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
In the morning I headed to the car rental place and found that they could only swap the car and not just do a tyre replacement. So we ended up with a Hyundai Sonata, slightly bigger and black, the perfect oven to slow cook us while we drive through Death Valley.

Car sorted we grabbed some breakfast and then got on our way to San Francisco, with a plan to stop just before the Golden Gate Bridge and visit Sausalito's Marine Mammal Center. The views along the coast just got better and better and as we neared the bridge we stopped off at Point Bonita Lighthouse just outside of San Francisco Bay where we could see seals bobbing around in the water below and sunbathing on rocks just off shore.

At the Marine Mammal Center we saw volunteers feeding the numerous seal pups that have been rescued. Volunteers helped with everything including the mountains of paperwork for each 'patient' as all manner of details are meticulously recorded in a patient file.

Before the much anticipated bridge crossing we stopped off at one more place, a viewing spot just north of the bridge, to get as many more pictures as we could. The parking area was overflowing with most people just leaving their car on the side of the road and walking down to the best vantage point.

Driving south over the bridge we could see back to Point Bonita Lighthouse out to the west, and make out most of the bay area over to the east. There is a $6 toll driving over the bridge in to San Francisco, but not coming out. Much like the toll to get in to Wales, but with less chance of rain.

Arriving at our hotel we were advised to empty our car as their parking was in a public car park a block away. They kindly offered to store our snowboarding bags in their warehouse at the back of the hotel so it worked out ok. The hotel was really quirky with plants everywhere and vines growing around all the exposed pipework on the ceilings. The rooms in the centre of the hotel with no exterior windows, instead had windows on to the hallways. All this made it feel like you were outside when walking along the corridors, but you had carpet under your feet. Each floor also had groupings of shared bathrooms, and an electric massage chair and bookcase, should you want to sit and read while the chair pokes you in the back. It wasn't very comfortable.

We walked down the two or three blocks to Fishermans Wharf and were suddenly in the thick of a busy Sunday evening in San Francisco. Pier 39 was chaotic with thousands of people swarming around, street entertainers and food stalls everywhere. We walked the length of it, usually at a snails pace behind queues of people, but it was amazing to see so much activity. Venturing further round the bay and down toward the Ferry Building, things got a little quieter and we decided to look for somewhere to eat. Out of the three or four places available one had a huge queue, so we knew it was probably good, but were too impatient to wait for it. After much indecision we decided to eat at a place called Slanted Door. It turned out to be fantastic food and we walked, stomachs full, back to our hotel. Only getting lost once.

The next day we hired bikes and were going to cycle over the Golden Gate Bridge to Sausalito, stop for a coffee and snack, and then catch a ferry back to the Ferry Building for a short cycle back to the rental shop. Having seen Lombard Street on the map though, I thought we should cycle down it as our car was secured for the four days we were here. Three grueling hills later we were at the top of the crookedest street in the world, along with twenty or so other people all busily taking pictures. We took ours and then waited for a gap in the cars winding their way down, to set off on the bikes. At the bottom we took more pictures with an even bigger crowd, and then made our way to the beginning of the bike path over the bridge.

The view was fantastic as we cycled over, and the wind wasn't as strong as I thought it would be. The bridge is unfortunately a well known spot for those wanting to end it all, but we were still a little surprised to see 'crisis phones' placed every so often along the walkway. Having climbed a few more hills to get on to the bridge, the road in to Sausalito was thankfully all downhill, back to just above sea level. After a snack we decided we could save $20 by cycling back across the bridge rather than catching the ferry, a decision which turned out to be questionable at best.

The beautiful winding road down in to Sausalito was now a twisty uphill ride back to the beginning of the bridge. In the two hours we'd spent in Sausalito, some clouds had gathered in the sky, covering up the sun, and there was now a strong crosswind to contend with on the bridge crossing. It didn't stop us though and we made it back to the rental shop looking forward to handing the bikes back in.

Back in our room we checked out the hotel offer of discounted food in the restaurant below. We made it with thirty minutes to spare for happy hour, and got half price drinks before discovering pasta dishes were also on half price. A cheap dinner to compensate for the slighly over budget meal the night before.

The next day we started a little later, don't shout at me but I woke up early and watched the Chinese Grand Prix as I'd heard it was a good one. It was! Yes I know I should be concentrating on where we are in the world. Once Nat was up and had finished her yoga we headed down to Pier 33 to catch a ferry across to Alcatraz. We were really excited about going to the island and seeing the prison. Included in the ticket was a 45 minute audio tour to guide you around and talk about the famous prisoners of Alcatraz and the many escape attempts. It was fascinating to learn so much about the prison, but one of the most interesting things was the community that lived on the island while it was a maximum security facility. All the prison staff were permanent residents in purpose built accommodation, with their wives and children living there as well. Children would catch a ferry back to the mainland for school each day, and not worry that they lived next door to some of the most dangerous people in America.

Back on the mainland we grabbed some food at Fog City Diner and then walked up to Pier 39, to see the sea lions we had missed on our first evening here. We caught a streetcar back to Ferry Building and then walked along Columbus through the heart of downtown, to Chinatown, the largest outside of China.

On our final day we checked out and drove to Alamo Square to capture a few pictures of the 'Painted Ladies', a group of seven well maintained Victorian houses that feature on a number of postcards, those that aren't of the bridge. Thinking our luck was in, we then headed for the Academy of Sciences in the Golden Gate Park. The third Thursday of each month was free so we were in for a treat. On arrival we were greeted with a queue hundreds of people long. It would take hours for us to get in, time we decided we didn't have. A bit dissapointed we thought it best to get on our way as the final few days of our stay in the USA were looking jam packed.
