---
title: "Whangarei and Poor Knights Islands"
date: 2010-06-04 01:29:00
author: "Natalie"
---
So our final morning in Auckland ran less than smoothly!

We had to check out of our room by ten am so did that and left all our stuff in a hallway of the hostel, didn't feel greatly confident about that but it seemed that everyone else's stuff was there too so went with it!

Arrived at the backpacker rental place to pick up the van, did the final paperwork, paid up, then while checking over the van we noticed a couple of things broken that you could put up with for a weeks rental but not two months! So they got us another van, this only took about ten minutes so was ok.

Checked around this one and it all looked good, better than the other one. We turned off the engine while we were looking round it to save fuel then when we tried to drive off nothing happened. The girl came out and asked if we had turned off the engine, we said yes and she told us that the van had been sat for a little while so the battery had been flat when they got to it, bloody cheeky buggers!

She said they would change the battery (too right) and gave us keys to a car to go and pick up our bags from the hostel.

It was only once we got into the very compact, automatic Yaris something-or-other that we thought about the fact that we had to fit four quite large bags into it including a snowboard bag!

We set off anyway back to the hostel, we found our bags exactly where we left them and amazingly with a bit of Tetris packing we got them in. It's a good job the car was automatic as there wasn't any room to change gear with a board bag jutting between us!

When we got back to the rental place the battery was all fixed so we loaded up and after one more stop for supplies we set off on our adventure.

We didn't drive for too long before we realised we were starving so pulled over by a beach and jumped in the back to make sarnies. It's so cool having everything we need right here and it's good to drive too. I hope I'm still saying this after fifty-six nights in it!

The van's from this company are all named after someone who works there and ours is called Mike Vicki (obviously named after two people) it has seats and a table that change into a bed, a gas hob and grill, fridge, sink and enough storage to hide all our bags away. We have even stored our snowboarding gear under one of the seats!

Setting off again we headed North on Highway one, the scenery all the way along our route was amazing and it is hard to keep your eyes on the road. Thank god there aren't as many pull in points as the US or we would never have got to Whangarei. On our way to the Blue Heron campsite we drove around a headland which has amazing views of nearby mountains, we stopped to take some more pics before driving to our pitch for the night.

At the campsite a lady called Elaine greeted us and showed us to the best site right by the waters edge, there was only one other van there so we had the run of the place.

After settling into the van a bit we took advantage of the kitchen and made some food and used the free wifi. Then it was back to the van to spend our first night in the van.

Not a bad first night, it was a bit chilly and there was an amazing amount of light coming in from the very bright full moon that was high in the sky.

Today was a bit of a let down as a lot of things we wanted to do were either fully booked (Zion Wildlife Park due to a memorial for a former ranger who was killed by one of the Lions), closed for the weekend (Native Bird Recovery Centre) or too wet and in a bit of a dodgy area to leave the van ( Abby Caves - home of stalagmites, stalactites and glowworms).

One place we did get to see was Whangarei Falls, it's only five kms from the city but feels a lot further out. After walking down to the Falls and back we took the opportunity for a snack in the van before heading to the nearby Tutukaka Coast which is so small we drove past it first time round!

Once we got back there we drove down to the Marina which is pretty much all there is there. The main reason people come here is to get out to the Marine Reserve of Poor Knights Islands and go scuba diving.

We went into a place that had been recommended, simply called Dive Tutukaka and got a quote. It was a bit pricey so we went outside to have a think about it but soon decided we don't know when would we get this opportunity again to dive at one of the top ten place in the world so to go for it and live off beans on toast for a while!

We went back in to book onto the next days boat, the weather looked really good and met some of the guys. The whole team seem really nice and professional and although I felt a bit nervous I really wanted to do it.

We asked the girl helping us where we could camp for the night without having to go to a site and she recommended somewhere called Wellington Bay not far away which had toilets and was right on the beach so we headed there.

Free-camping is frowned upon in some parts of NZ, I think it used to be more acceptable before the now huge amounts of people descended into the country, picked up camper vans and drove all over it. You have to make sure you ask a local so we were quite confident that this place would be ok. It was a really nice setting and we got there as the sun was going down. Went for a walk on the beach before positioning the van on a grassy bank in front of the beach and not in view of any houses.

We did our first nights cooking in the van which was obviously exiting then got our stuff ready for the next day.

Once we got into bed we did spend a while listening to each car that drove past to see if they were going to stop and ask us to move off but nothing happened and we must have fallen asleep soon after.

Had such an early start that the sun wasn't even around when we got up. Got ready and drove off leaving no trace that we'd even been there, well apart from the slight skid mark from the tyres!

Arrived at the Dive centre, got kitted up and went out to the boat. Once on board we all introduced ourselves, there was Luke, Sam and Kieran the Dive team, Toby and Hannah from Germany and Claire from the USA. We had a briefing and set off onto an almost calm Sea for our hour long journey out to the Poor Knights Islands.

There are two main volcanic Islands called Tawhiti Rahi and Aorangi, these Islands were home to a tribe called the Ngai Wai up until a massacre in the 1800's and since then they have been forbidden. Even now the public are banned and the only people allowed on the Islands are scientists who are studying the pristine environment and unique animals and plant life that exist there. The Islands were established as a marine reserve in 1981 and are bathed in a subtropical current from the Coral Sea which means they are home to many varieties of tropical and non tropical marine life including Manta rays, Seals, Turtles and many types of fish.

I was feeling increasingly nervous on the way out to the Islands and Dan was becoming incredibly exited. I felt a bit better once we dropped anchor, the Sea was calm and the sky was blue. Not bad for winter. We had more of a briefing of the two dives we would do and who was taking who in a group. Then it was time to get the kit on and get in the water.

Getting into the water was easy, going under was a bit harder for me but as I relaxed into it and saw all the fish around us I started to enjoy myself. Dan was in his element and ended up using all his air up in about thirty minutes while the rest of us were down for about fifty!

In the first dive through amazingly clear water we saw Scorpion fish, a big Crayfish, shoal of Blue Mao Mao and lots of others I don't know the names of! We were told to look out for the Bronze Whaler Shark which are sometimes spotted around here but none were seen.

Once we were all back on the boat it was lunch time and hot drink time, the Sea temp was about nineteen degrees so not bad for us brits. Especially considering we did our PADI last winter!

The second dive was at a different location and was slightly shallower, this dive I enjoyed much more and we saw even more fish. Also because the water was shallower the sunlight was penetrating through the water to illuminate shoal of fish. We saw some pretty big fish this time and also some Moray Eels. We were underwater for longer this time, about sixty-five minutes!

It was an amazing dive. As we got dressed the guys drove to boat round to show us the biggest Sea cave in the world, then as we were leaving the Islands we spotted a few Seals that were messing around in the water.

On our way back to shore we had noticed that we seemed to be heading back in a slightly different direction when the skipper stopped the boat and announced that we had come a bit out of our way to see some Orca Whales that were passing through.

They were absolutely amazing and came right next to the boat! There was a crazy woman speaking Whale to them in a small boat and it was definitely working as they were going right up to her.

What a great way to finish our day everyone was on such a high seeing these amazing animals in their natural environment and still interacting with humans. That night we headed back to the Blue Heron to try again to get to Zion the next day.

Wow what an incredible day. We phoned Zion first thing and they were not booked at all so we drove over there to have a normal tour of the park. Dennis greeted us and began by walking us over to meet Mandla the resident Black Leopard, he was gorgeous and looked just like a big pussy cat who wanted fuss. Then we saw one of two Serval's at the park, called Silas and Zoe, much smaller cat's but with big ears. They had one elderly Baboon called Foxy who had been collected from a Circus years ago when the park rescued some cats she was in the package.

Next we saw two Cheetah's called Kenya and Thabo. They were amazing and after telling Dennis I used to have a bit of an obsession with Cheetah's he said I could upgrade to a Cheetah chat which meant I could feed and stroke them. I decided this I had to do but carried on with the rest of the tour first.

The next cats to see were the White Lions followed by the Bengal Tigers and the Royal White Tigers. It was great seeing all these cats in the flesh as we has seen them on tv and some of them we had seen as cubs and now they were fully grown Lions and Tigers.

Next along were the Barbary Lions who are among the rarest in the world. All the cats looked really relaxed apart from when one lot would start making noise then they would all join in and it would get a bit rowdy. Some of the female cats were in season so that made all the males horny and the other females jealous! It was great in a way to see them active and charging around as you could really see their power and potential.

The last cat to meet was Zion, he is an African Lion who has appeared in many adverts in NZ and is Aslan in the Narnia films!

So the final bit of the day was to go and have my Cheetah chat. We met Anton who would be taking me and he lead us back to the Cheetah enclosure with a couple of buckets of freshly cut meat and a baby bottle of milk formula.

Before we entered the enclosure he asked if I was nervous and to tell him if I did get nervous, he also just explained a couple of things to do if the cats react in a certain way.

I can honestly say I wasn't nervous, walking in the Cheetah's stamped their front paws and hissed a couple of times but they soon calmed down when they saw the food. Anton filled a bowl with the milk and the male Cheetah Kenya jumped up onto the flat rock, snarled a few times and started to drink the milk. Then Anton put some bits of meat in the bowl too and asked me to go round to him, he explained that when they are eating they are focused on that and you can stroke them and pretty much do anything to them and they don't mind.

So I started stroking him and he didn't flinch, just carried on devouring then bits of meat being put into his bowl. He really was a gorgeous animal, the fur was much denser than a normal cat.

Next I sat on a small rock on the ground and was passed bits of meat to feed the female Cheetah Thabo, she was a bit more nervous but came over and took the still warm meat from me. While she was eating it I stroked her too. She got a bit jumpy when the Lions and Tigers started getting rowdy again but a few gentle words from Anton to reassure her meant that she carried on eating.

I can't really explain the experience apart from that it was awesome. When I was doing it I just kept thinking what an amazing animal this was and it was wild and I was stroking it and then afterwards I couldn't believe it had just happened and my body was rushing with adrenaline. It felt a bit like an out of body experience!

On our way out of the park Anton stopped by the White Lions to say hello and got us some Mane hair to feel, it was coarse and a bit like the Cheetah's fur but much longer.

Anton had such a good relationship with the cats they really seemed to know him and greeted him fondly.

We said goodbye and left the park after signing the visitors book.

The park was really well run and you could tell that everyone here just loves all the animals that live here and want to see them survive and hopefully thrive rather than becoming extinct. I could probably just stay here and be a volunteer!

We left Zion and headed north making brief stops to get fuel, refill the gas bottle and check out some famous public loo's designed by a guy called Hundertwasser in a little place called Kawakawa, we made use of them, it'd be rude not to, then carried onto Pahia where we arrived in the dark and luckily found a convenient RV park for $15, thats just £7. Bargin!
