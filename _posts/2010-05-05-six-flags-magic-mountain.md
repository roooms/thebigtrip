---
title: "Six Flags Magic Mountain"
date: 2010-05-05 18:48:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
When we first decided to travel down the west coast of the United States I thought we should go to a big theme park. Something on a US scale that would make the likes of Alton Towers and Chessington feel quite small in comparison. I knew some of the roller coasters in the US would be bigger, faster and more intense than the best the UK had to offer, and after looking in to it a bit, Six Flags Magic Mountain was close to LA, our final stop before leaving the US.

We had to do some last minute shuffling and put our theme park experience behind Death Valley, Las Vegas and the Grand Canyon as otherwise we would be there on a Sunday, which concerned me in case the queues were massive. We re-arranged it to be there on a Thursday, hoping we wouldn't need to buy extra passes to jump queues.

The Motel 6 we stayed in kindly printed out our online tickets we bought in the morning to save some cash over the standard price you got on arrival, and once we got there it was looking promising that we would have a good day.

There was hardly anyone around, and while convincing Nat to go on the first big coaster 'Goliath' we started talking to a family there who said it was due to the cold weather that it was so empty. We were thankful that most Californians thought a mid-twenty degree day was still cold and got straight to the front of the first train.

I managed to go on all the big roller coasters apart from 'X2' (unfortunately my main reason for wanting to visit Magic Mountain) as it was too windy to open it. The ride of the day was definitely 'Tastu' though, which was very similar in design to Air at Alton Towers, Where you are rotated backwards in to a seated 'flying' position before the coaster starts. It was longer, faster and much higher though!

Nat joined me on any coaster that didn't have a loop in it, and her favourite of the day was 'Terminator: Salvation', a new wooden coaster that felt out of control as it raced around banked turns and twisted back over and under itself a number of times.

We went on a couple of water rides and proceeded to get soaked each time, but the sun would dry us off quickly enough. The longest queues were for 'Riddler's Revenge' a stand -up roller coaster and 'Tatsu'. Each time it was no more than about twenty minutes though.

The park wasn't as big as I had assumed it would be, I think Alton Towers is probably bigger thanks to the castle and gardens it has. But the quantity and quality of the big roller coasters was higher.

Next stop was the heart of LA for a few days before our flight to Rarotonga.
