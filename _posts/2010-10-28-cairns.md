---
title: "Cairns"
date: 2010-10-28 17:33:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
After our early hours flight from Darwin we arrived in Cairns just after 8am. We hadn’t booked any accommodation and decided to just try our luck when we got there. This sort of worked, when we managed to book in to a cheap room at a backpackers called ‘Asylum’ which also offered free airport pick-up. I say sort of, because at check-in we realised we were quite a walk from the main centre of Cairns, and the backpackers was a bit run-down compared to the others we had seen available. We ended up in a double room with a fan that had only two working settings; off and tornado and an air-con unit fitted in one of the windows that made the whole wall shake when it was turned on. We booked in for two nights and after dropping our stuff in the room, headed in to the heart of Cairns to see how much of it Nat could remember.

Like Darwin, Cairns has a salt water lagoon where everyone can play in the water without the fear of crocs eyeing them up as a mid-day snack. It’s even more important in Cairns though as there aren’t actually any beaches, croc-infested or otherwise; just a lot of grass and mud leading down to the waters edge. The main road along the waterfront was full of hostels (that we probably should have stayed in instead) and food courts, and there was a casino surrounded by up-market hotels near the waters edge.

Not that keen on cooking in the kitchen at the backpackers, we grabbed $10 meals at a bar called ‘The Woolshed’ on our first night. We had a quiet one after our alarm went off at something ridiculous like 3am that morning.

The next day was spent lying on the grass by the lagoon, with the occasional dip to cool off. Before heading back to the hostel we stopped in at the bus terminal and grabbed two hop-on, hop-off tickets from Cairns to Sydney. It allowed us ninety days to make our way south, although we’d be doing it in three weeks. That evening we found out that our friend Sasa who we met on the Western Xposure tour, had arrived in Cairns and we planned to meet up for Noodles later in the evening. Sasa knew Cairns really well and we had great food at a little place just outside of the centre, then dropped in on the casino, not for gambling, but for the free hot drinks served there!

The following morning we checked out of our room and relaxed by the lagoon, which was conveniently close to the main bus terminal where our Greyhound was waiting to take us on the first leg of our journey down the east coast. The first stint would be a six hour ride to Townsville.
