Arrived in Kaikoura after a few stops to snap some more amazing views. It was a nice enough place but pretty sleepy. We went and had lunch with yet another Seal colony before getting on our way.

The next stop was at a place called Hanmer Springs which uses thermal water for it's many spa pools of varying temperatures. It was a bit pricey but we went for it as at least we could get a shower out of it too! We worked our way between temperatures of 36 degrees and 41 degrees, this was a sulpher pool and we didn't stay in it long as it reminded us of Rotorua! After an hour of boiling ourselves we got clean and set off in the dark to our free campsite for the night.

Leaving Hanmer Springs in the dark was fine but it meant that the petrol station had closed for the night, we had a quarter of a tank of fuel left and I was assured that we would have enough. As we passed a campsite on the outskirts of town I wondered if we should just pay and stay there the night in order to fill up in the morning but we motored on.

The weather deteriorated rapidly as we drove and soon we were being battered by wind and rain whilst trying to stay on an increasingly windy road, then the petrol light flickered into action. Great.

I found myself swearing that we would never let the tank get below half if we could just get to the fuel station before running out, the only good thing was that the light was still going out as we went downhill so there was still some fuel sloshing around in the tank!

Finally we got to the free campsite that we had been planning on staying at, I pulled over and we looked at the entrance. It was a gravel/mud slope into the site and going on our earlier feat of trying to get off the campsite after a night of rain we decided against it and carried on steadily to the garage.

I don't think I've ever been more pleased to see a neon garage sign, we pulled up, it was closed but you could do credit card payment. It was also one of the more expensive prices so we just put enough in to keep us going and carried on 6 kms to another campsite which we were supposed to pay $12 for but didn't bother because of the short amount of time we were spending there.

I think if I had known what a nightmare journey it was going to be it would have just been worth paying for a night on a site and saving the stressful drive, oh well you travel and learn!

Woke up to a wet morning, packed up the van and left for Nelson. It rained nearly all the way there but then all of a sudden it stopped and we had lovely sunshine for the end of our journey. It was as if we just drove out from under the raincloud.

Arrived in Nelson and had a wander around, it was quite busy and was nice to reconnect with the world and do some little bits in town.

We treated ourselves to a powered site which was needed as the night drew in and the sky was clear and cold.

Due to the clear night the morning was fresh and we had a gorgeous blue sky to get up to. We headed back into Nelson and stocked up on food and fuel, our next stop was the Abel Tasman National Park where there isn't much of anything except lovely beaches and forest so we decided to be prepared. Once we had got some useful info on the area such as pubs and beaches to visit we started our drive.

It was so nice to be driving in good weather, we made good time and arrived in Golden Bay ready for a lunch stop. We pulled into a place called Pupu Springs which is a natural cool water spring. It pumps around 40 baths worth of water a second out of the deep rock. The spring is sacred to the Maori and so clear that you can see right to the bottom which is seven metres down.

We chilled out there for a bit and had lunch before carrying on to our destination for the next two nights at Totaranui Beach.

After a 10km drive down a gravel track, arriving at the beach was really exciting. There was no-one else there, the weather was still fab and the sand on the beach was like gold! We wandered around for a bit after choosing the best place to put the van then Dan decided to make a fire in the fire pit we handily parked next to. Unfortunately it was unsucessful and I had to stop him before he used all the matches up on it!

The trouble with these lovely days is that the nights are pretty much freezing and tonight we had no power so we went to bed wearing the most clothes yet, this consisted of thermals (top and bottoms), three pairs of socks (one of them snowboarding socks), tracksuit bottoms, t shirt, hoodie, hat and gloves!

After having a restless nights sleep during which I couldn't decide if I was too hot or too cold we woke up to rain. This was not what we had planned for and ended up not getting dressed and staying in the van all day waiting for it to clear up which didn't happen. It was probably the worst day yet and left us feeling a bit down and homesick. We could have left the site but we decided to wait it out and see if it cleared for the next day.

Luckily after a better nights sleep we woke up to a windy but bright day which lifted our spirits and made us feel good about staying. We set off to do a tramp through some of the forest to the next couple of bays along from ours. It was so nice to be out and about and breathing fresh air rather than being stuck in the van!

After lunch we drove back to Golden Bay, picked up some essential items which amounted to a lighter, two pies and a pack of Afghan biscuits (not as good as Ali's!) and drove to the northern most point you can drive to in the South Island.

We arrived at Wharariki parked the van and after a twenty minute walk through fields we arrived at a desert like scene. Lots of sand dunes and grasses growing wildly. As we walked towards the sea the wind was strong and was blowing the sand along which looked like stripes running to the sea. It was a great beach and we caught the start of a pink sunset. After walking back and getting sandblasted we went into a holiday park right next to where the walk to the beach started. It was a new place, so new that there were still diggers around creating the landscape!

We took up our site and went to check out the very 'green' facilities. It was a great site(although the wind continued and rocked the van all night), we were the only people there and they were doing a lot to stay as eco as possible. The only dowside to that was that the showers cost $2 for 5 minutes, 4 minutes for the boys! Needless to say Dan showered in the ladies in the morning.

Left the still windy Wharariki and headed to our next recommendation, The Mussel Inn. This was a really cosy bar/resturant where we settled by the open fire and enjoyed a couple of home-made pies washed down with a glass of Manuka beer for Dan and a glass of Sauv Blanc for me. It was really quiet in there but you could tell by all the stacked up speakers and photos on the wall that when they get bands in then this is the place to be. We could have stayed for the rest of the day but had to make a move so we stocked up on some locally made chocolates and set off on a fairly big drive south.

So our long drive turned into a slightly longer one as the first site we were due to stay at had unbearable toilets, think Big Chill on the last day!

Then the next place we got to was shut because roadworks were blocking the entrance. By this point we had to swap driving and Dan took over to get us to Westport which we arrived at only four and a half hours after leaving Golden Bay!

We found a campsite which was still open, booked a powered site and indulged ourselves with internet and the chocolates we had bought earlier.

It felt good to be back in the world of technology and free showers!
