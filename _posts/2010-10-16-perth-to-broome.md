This section of the blog may be a bit long but bear with me because it is all about our ten day tour from Perth to Broome and we did a lot of cool stuff that I really want you to hear about. I’m going to try to explain it all in one go, maybe you should get a nice cuppa or glass of vino before you start!

**Day 1.**

We were up early and down the road from our hostel for our pick up. It was a bit chilly and we were both looking forward to getting some warmer weather up the coast.

We got to the busy tour depot and were sorted in to heavy duty off road buses. On the bus our tour guide for the next ten days introduced himself, Chris and the bus, Wombat. We had the health and safety chat and rules of the Wombat and then we were off waving goodbye to sunny Perth.

We were sat at the back of the bus which was quite bumpy and after an hour I had to move towards the front of the bus to avoid the dreaded travel sickness. My new neighbour was called Sasa from Taiwan.

The first task I had to do was read out everyone’s horoscope from the paper in my best posh newsreader English accent. Then we all had to stand at the front of the Wombat and tell everyone our names and where we were from and a little bit about ourselves. This went pretty well and was a good way to start to get to know this group of people that we would end up being with pretty much 24/7 for the next 10 days.

Our first stop was unscheduled and involved Chris pulling onto the side of the road and running back down the road, after a couple of minutes he came back with a Bob-tail Lizard to show us A few of us had a hold (including me of course) before he was released back into the wild.

Our next stop was scheduled and was the Pinnacles at Nambung National Park, these strange rock formations are made from limestone and are caused by the erosion of the earth that they used to be a part of. We had a wander through these and chatted to two of the girls on our trip, Laura and Caitriona. We went a bit off track because of the chatting but managed to find our way back just as Chris was coming to round us all up.

Next stop was lunch by the beach then shortly after we went sand boarding which was awesome. Most of us had a go, Dan did a couple of runs standing up then we waxed the boards and had a race. The finale was a running race, first one to run up the sand dune and back down would win some beer. We stayed out of this one but of course videoed it and managed to catch Rob winning but also face planting into the sand!

This managed to tire us out ready for a fairly big drive to our first nights accommodation which was a nice homely hostel in Horrocks. We bundled all our stuff into our dorms and went to start cooking whilst watching an amazing sunset over the Indian Ocean, which we toasted with a couple of bottles of yummy Strongbow!

Spent the evening chatting to everyone and trying to remember names which is pretty hard when there are 17 of them! The majority of the group were German girls, there was a handful of Irish girls, one other English girl plus an Austrian guy, a Taiwanese girl, American guy and a Scotsman.

**Day 2.**

After an early start and a big breakfast, the bright moon was still out and we were off to our first stop of the day, the Murchison Gorge in Kalbarri National Park. We took a fairly taxing walk down a track called the z-bend which took us to a small river within the gorge. On the way back we had to dodge some angry bees which had been disturbed by another group.

We walked back up to a lookout and got our first group picture before going to check out a natural hole in the rock called Nature’s Window.

Luckily then it was lunchtime and we were all ready for a sit down and some refreshment, the temperature was definitely heating up.

We set off on our next long haul and at some point on the journey we thought we had run over something, we told Chris but he said it was fine. When we arrived at the Billabong Roadhouse for refreshments Chris noticed that he had a flat on one of the tyres so our stop turned out to be a bit longer than planned. Dan and Rob helped Chris while me, Laura and Caitriona did the sensible thing and went next door to the outback pub for a beer, well it was 36 degrees in the shade!

The boys did a grand job and after an hour we were on our way, we managed to get to Shell beach which is as the name suggests a beach made of tiny white shells in which we buried Laura. Dan and some of the girls went for a swim before getting back on the Wombat and getting to our overnight stop at Denham.

We had a slap up barbie of steak and sausages with salad washed down with a couple of JD+coke cans we had picked up at the bottle shop. A few of us sat up and played some drinking and card games.

**Day 3.**

Another early start saw us heading to Monkey Mia, this is where wild Dolphins turn up every day for feeding which they have been doing for around the last 50 years and only turn up if and when they want to. The day we were there happened to be school holidays and we could already see some Dolphins swimming around and there was a huge crowd of people forming. As they do three feedings per morning Chris advised us not to do the first feeding but to chill in the coffee shop and wait for the second one. A few of us did as he said but most of the group joined the throngs of people cramming the shore to see the Dolphins.

After the first feeding we went down to the beach to chat to the rest of our group and Laura had been picked to feed one of the Dolphins! She was buzzing with excitement and I was looking forward to the second feeding. We had to wait a while as the Dolphins dictate when the feedings happen but eventually a couple turned up and were playing around in the shallow water, we even got to see some jumps. The lady who organises the feedings came down and was chatting about the Dolphins and as she did the Dolphins were darting around in the shallows, we weren’t sure what they were doing till the lady said they were catching their own fish. This was really nice to see and proved that they were still wild and hadn’t been trained at all. One of the Dolphins was even coming up and showing off that he had caught his own fish, very cool!

In the end one mother Dolphin and her calf turned up for the feeding and a child got picked to do it, we were really happy to have seen them playing and acting naturally.

Our next stop was Eagle Bluff which is part of the world heritage site, Shark Bay. Eagle Bluff is an amazing lookout over clear waters in which we could just make out the dark shapes of what we presumed to be Sharks.

We trucked on to a marine reserve called Hamlin Pool which is the home to a colony of Stromatolites, these brown rocklike formations are not the most exciting looking things but they are the reason we are all here as they were the first living things on Earth.

After lunch it was time for a monster 5 hour drive, I sat right up front with Matt who had organised a hilarious game to keep the gang happy for a bit.

It was great sitting up front, you see so much more like Emu’s and massive road trains and of course a lot of road kill, boo.

Just as the sun was dipping below the horizon we stopped at the signpost marking the Tropic of Capricorn which we used as a toilet/stretch/photo opportunity.

We eventually arrived at Coral Bay which is part of the Ningaloo Marine Park and lugged our stuff into our rooms,. We got free burgers which were much needed and got some cheap drinks. Settled into some card games including ring of fire which included a rule where we had to name everyone around the table which was great as we finally learnt each others names so here’s the run down The German girls: Verena, Alena, Claudia, Kristina, Saskia, Vanessa, Stephanie, Bridget. The Irish girls: Orla, Deirdre (Dee), Clodagh (Clo), Caitriona (Cat). Laura from London, Rob the American, Sasa from Taiwan, Matt the Austrian and Mark the Scotsman. Phew.

A few of us took the party to the Wombat where we could be as noisy as we liked

**Day 4.**

Today we went on our Manta Ray tour, headed out to the reef on a fab boat. The weather was gorgeous and the sea was clear but a bit cold. We soon forgot about that once we were snorkelling and checking out the coral and fish that were around, Dan saw a Turtle and got some good footage and out of nowhere a reasonable sized White Tip Reef Shark turned up chasing a fish! This was so exciting and a bit scary the kind of thing that you makes you want to swim away but you can’t because you are transfixed.

Back on the boat it was time for snacks and hot drinks while we motored over to a Turtle sanctuary, we saw some of them coming up for air occasionally.

Then it was time to find the main event of the day, there was a plane overhead looking for the Manta Rays which soon located them. We had been split into smaller groups to snorkle with the rays, I was in the first group and took the underwater camera. Getting in the water was really exciting as we had to be quick but not make too much splash, as soon as we were in we had to swim like mad to keep up with our guide Albie but soon we reached him and the beautiful, graceful Manta Rays. There were five of them circling a cleaning station, we stayed above them and watched them for what felt like ages but I think was only about ten minutes.

My group got back on the boat then it was Dan’s group’s turn, after the third group we moved to a different area, had a big lunch and chatted about the amazing experience we had just been a part of.

After lunch it was time for our final snorkle, there were quite a few fish around and some of the group saw a pretty big shark but unfortunately we didn’t.

Our boat headed back to harbour and we got a little look at a huge fish called Merv, he was a type of grouper or wrasse and was probably about a metre long!

Back at base in Coral Bay we checked out the photos of the trip and one of us bought the cd to share around, then it was back to the Wombat for another big drive this time to Exmouth.

As we were getting into Exmouth Chris pulled the bus over, shouted to Dan and jumped out. Dan had already been told about what was going on and so had I. We all got off and ran towards a newly built bridge over a river, by the time we got there Chris was up on the railings and then jumped off, Dan was next then I decided to strip down to my bikini and do the jump too. It was an eight metre drop into unexpectedly salty water, great though. We had to run back to the bus as we shouldn’t have been doing what we were doing especially with a big Western Exposure bus (sorry Wombat) parked up!

That evening we took some drinks to the beach for a bit of a gathering and later on ended up playing cards, good times.

**Day 5.**

The pace really slowed down on day five which was much needed for everyone, we had done so many K’s so everyone enjoyed having a day at the beach. We went to Turquoise Bay and did some sunbathing and swimming then went for a fairly challenging drift snorkle, saw lots of fish and loads of amazing coral. I saw a stingray and some of the guys saw a couple of sharks and a turtle so quite successful. After lunch we had a bit more time on the beach before dropping off two of our group to a different hotel as they were ending their tour there and then heading back to the hostel for uber quick showers and getting back out to go on a whale watching tour.

The boat was lovely, we had snacks provided and had brought a few drinks on with us and best of all we saw lots of action from the humpback whales making their migratory journey. We also spied a few dolphins and a sea snake swam right past the boat.

Watched a beautiful sunset then it was time to head back to shore.

**Day 6.**

Another early one as we had a long driving day ahead of us but we were really excited as we were off to Karijini. We stopped for lunch at a place called Tom Price which was the last bit of civilisation before entering the outback. We all stocked up on snacks just in case and set off.

At about 4pm we arrived at our eco camping ground, there were tents, a bbq and stove already set up for us. As soon as we arrived we went for a walk to a nearby gorge called Joffre’s, this was a nice little gorge and there were a few places where Chris, Dan, Kristina and Saskia did a few jumps then we went to check out the waterfall. The sun was about to set so we set off back to camp.

We made good use of the bbq and had a few beers while we played some card games. Then Chris got us playing the cornflake box game, you know the one where you all have to pick up a cornflake box with your teeth then a strip gets ripped off the top of it each time you have all had a go so it gets lower and lower to the ground.

We all turned out to be pretty flexible and Dan made it to the final with me, Cat, Kristina, Rob and Chris. Rob ended up being the winner by picking up the flat piece of cardboard whilst standing on a beam, Mr soft!

After exhausting ourselves we grabbed our swags and sleeping bags and headed out to an area of bush where there were no lights. We got cosy in our swags and looked up in amazement to how many stars were in the sky, the more our eyes adjusted the more we could see and then we started seeing shooting stars too. Just before going to sleep we watched a plane gliding across the sky and then heard the engine noise catch up with it, amazing.

**Day 7.**

Had a great nights sleep and woke up to see a beautiful red glow on the horizon just before the sun came up. The early morning air was fresh but it wasn’t long before we could feel the heat of the day, by eight am you could already feel it heating up!

Had a big breakfast as we were off to do three gorges today.

The gorges we did were called Knox, Weano and Hancock, all day we were walking, looking at waterfalls, climbing, spider walking, swimming and jumping into freshwater pools. It was fantastic to be in that environment the scenery was awesome and we all worked together as a team to help each other out.

We finished the day at Oxers lookout where we could lookout over all the gorges we had been in and out of.

As you can imagine we were all exhausted and after our yummy spicy curry we had a couple of beers and took to our swags under the starry night sky.

**Day 8.**

Didn’t have such a good nights sleep but we were up with the sun anyway and off to more gorges although at a more chilled pace. We drove to Dales gorge, took a steep path down into the gorge then walked along a pathway till we reached circular pool. This was a gorgeous place to swim and we found a few places to jump in too! Next Chris left us to walk through the gorge to the next pool while he went to move the Wombat.

We arrived at Fortescue Falls where Chris was waiting for us and carried onto the sacred fern pool, this was another stunning pool with a cool tree branch in it to play on and to use as an aid to get to the bottom of the pool.

We wandered back to Fortescue for a dip and a sunbathe on some hot rocks before it was time to climb back out of the gorge.

Back at camp we had another bbq and cracked open some more beers, we were just getting ready to play the so far avoided game of blindfold karaoke (singing to a song with your ipod on and full blast and a blindfold so only you can hear the music and everyone else can hear you!) when Chris got called out to help another bus that had broken down on its way into Karijini. Phew!

Luckily he wasn’t gone too long and when he got back we celebrated by doing shotguns with cans of beer.

Cat and me learnt the German alphabet thanks to Saskia and I am pleased to say I can still recite and we never got round to playing the game that we had all been dreading.

Our last night sleeping under the stars, Dee and Clo and me went to sleep in the gentlemen’s club which was a raised platform with a roof on it just for a bit more comfort.

I went to sleep thinking I would miss sleeping outside.

**Day 9.**

On our last morning after a few yoga stretches on the platform we went to a final gorge called Kalimina, we had a swim in the small pool and dried off in the morning sun. We said goodbye to three of our group who were heading back down to Perth. We waved Rob, Matt and Claudia off then shortly after we walked back up out of the gorge and got onto the Wombat for our trip to Port Headland.

Had a stop at a roadhouse and then picked up an extra passenger. Katie was a friend of Chris’s who had spent three months working on a farm at Marble Bar (the hottest place in Oz) to get her second year visa. She brought a case of beers onto the bus so we cracked into those and trucked onto our stop for the night at a cattle station called Pardoo.

We saw some kangaroos on our way into the station which were some of the only live ones I had seen on the trip.

We all settled by the pool and carried on with the beers which of course ended up with a couple of people being thrown in.

Got some food on and then played a few games of werewolf which was fun and got a few people riled up before bed.

**Day 10.**

Our last early start and we were a bit late which didn’t impress the boss but he was ok after he got an energy drink into him. We stopped at a roadhouse which also had a pure white peacock.

We drove to a campsite called Barn Hill Beachside Station Stay which had an amazing empty beach and a gorgeous warm sea to have a dip in. We had a chill on the beach for a bit before going back to the site to make lunch.

During our final drive to Broome Chris got us to write something nice or funny about each other on a piece of paper which then got read out by Cat. This was a really nice thing to do and everyone got to keep their comments.

Arriving in Broome Chris gave us a tour and we had one last group picture by Cable beach before he dropped us off at our respective backpackers.

We had the best time, met some great new friends and loved Western Australia.
