---
title: "Seattle to the slopes"
date: 2010-03-03 20:01:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
We caught the train from Seattle to Vancouver on Monday and travelled along an amazing coastal rail route, dipping in to the countryside every now and then on a very relaxing four hour journey. Coach class was very comfortable with big reclining seats and power sockets for using/charging all the electrical kit we brought with us. We crossed the border right next to the [Peace Arch](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Arch) monument. An inscription on the U.S. side of the Peace Arch reads "Children of a common mother", and one on the Canadian side reads "Brethren dwelling together in unity". All is fair in love and hockey.

When we arrived in Vancouver we had to pass through immigration where they seemed a little shocked we would be spending so long in Canada. A month of snowboarding seemed a long time to us as well but I'm sure it's nothing out of the ordinary really. We were let through and had a short wait until boarding a [Greyhound](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_bus) to take us the rest of the way to Whistler. A more cramped two and a half hours later and we arrived in a rainy village that was being torn apart following what looked like a very exciting Olympics.

Long story short, we were told the chalet we had booked and fully paid for was unavailable due to money not changing hands between the booking company and the chalet owner. We had temporary accommodation we could use until this had been resolved. Not ideal after the long journey. Added to the rain and everyone we met saying the spark had gone since the Olympics finished, left us feeling a bit down. After a few drinks and some great food at a place called [La Bocca](http://labocca.moonfruit.com/) we came to our senses and were determined to enjoy ourselves and not worry about where we were staying.

That brings us to today, our first day on the slopes of Whistler, and as it happened (by accident) Blackcomb. We carefully planned our first route of the day and jumped straight on the wrong lift at the village. It took us half way up Blackcomb instead of Whistler (no bad thing really), and so we worked our way across and up until we were at the top. A few runs later and we decided to catch the Peak 2 Peak Gondola across to Whistler mountain and work our way back down from there. We got to ride on all types of snow from powder to ice to slush on the way down, stumbling across [Beavertails](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver_tail_(pastry)) on the last run before the bottom.

Back at the village and we walked the streets for a bit checking out what sales were on, before stopping for a beer and bite to eat opposite La Bocca. Check out the pictures for the biggest plate of Nachos we have ever seen! We also found out we could move to our original chalet and have spent the evening unpacking and meeting the current residents. Tomorrow will be hot tub testing day!
