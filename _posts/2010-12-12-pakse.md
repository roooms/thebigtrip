---
title: "Pakse"
date: 2010-12-12 22:21:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
There was a certain degree of chaos at the bus station, but after getting our tickets exchanged for very similar looking tickets but with a bus registration on, we waited for bus 5999 to arrive. As it pulled in it really was a sight to behold. Glowing from front to back in bright neon colours, it declared itself ‘King of Bus’ and we humbly climbed aboard and found our double bed at the front. Our bed along with the one opposite were slightly elevated so a second mattress could lay beneath it with just enough space to squeeze additional bus drivers in when they had to group up on long journeys.

We got comfortable and as we set off, staff came round with snacks and water that were included in the bus fare. Nat managed to get to sleep quite quickly; I was engrossed in another Harry Bosch novel having been given one some time ago by one of the girls on the Western Xposure tour and getting hooked. It was probably our easiest long distance journey yet. The time seemed to sail past and we both got some sleep. At 6.45am we pulled in to the bus station at Pakse and hopped in a car to the city centre.

We grabbed some breakfast before finding a room and ended up in Thalaung Guesthouse, home to an aviary of birds and some flying squirrels. The room didn’t come with hot water but it did come with cable TV. Good to see they have their priorities in order. The rest of the day was a bit of a right-off. We took a quick wander but I started to feel a bit faint and not long after getting back to our room I was really ill. The rest of the day was spent watching CNN and it wasn’t until the next morning that I could happily stand up without losing my eyesight or feeling sick.

Feeling slightly better following a nights sleep we stopped in at a restaurant called Delta Coffee for brunch, before walking to Wat Tham Fai and getting a tuk-tuk back to Wat Luang, the two biggest temples in Pakse. My hearing was fading in and out so we were taking it easy. We took a stroll down to the rivers edge but Pakse was proving to be a very quiet place with little in the way of shops or stalls. After buying our bus tickets to Si Phan Don and stopping in an internet café to update the blog, we hopped on a tuk-tuk to Clinic Keo Ou Done a few kilometres out of the city centre, where we hoped to get a good Lao massage. There was a quick stop at the Keo Ou Done bus terminal as the driver wasn’t entirely sure where we wanted to go, but we eventually found the small sauna and massage clinic. We both had a traditional Lao massage which included a herbal tea at the end. Our friendly tuk-tuk driver was back in time to pick us up for the return trip to our guesthouse.

Pakse was one of the quietest urbanised places we have been, even though it is supposed to be a hub for travel around Southern Laos. If you didn’t have a day locked in the bathroom, one night here would be enough.
