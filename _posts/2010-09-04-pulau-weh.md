After a shorter than expected ferry crossing we arrived at Balohan, the ferry port of Pulau Weh and were immediately surrounded by taxi drivers. I think we were pretty much chosen by one of the few becak drivers and after agreeing on a good price we were squeezed onto the bike’s sidecar with our bags and we were away.

It was a great way to travel although the seats are not made for western bums, even those that have lost a bit of extra padding!

The road to Gapang beach was smooth, a bit twisty and we found ourselves leaning forward in the sidecar to help the bike up the steeper hills, the scenery was amazing with thick green jungle and occasional glimpses of turquoise ocean. We saw a few cows wandering along the road and quite a big Monitor lizard sunning himself on the tarmac, we had close encounters with a few large bugs including butterfly’s the size of small birds so kept our sunnies on and our mouths closed!

We arrived at Lumba Lumba safe and sound and checked in at the dive centre which was also our accommodation for the next few nights, we signed up for the afternoon dive and went to check out the area. Lumba Lumba was set back from the beach a few metres then there were various restaurants all along the beach, it had a really relaxed vibe and everyone there was just chilling and there for the diving and snorkelling.

We got back to LL and checked into our room, it was really spacious and had a big balcony. Went to get all our dive kit together and then we were on the boat and off to do our first dive in the Indian/Andaman Ocean. We went to a dive site called Rubiah Sea Garden, I was a bit nervous but didn’t have much time to think about it as we were soon off the boat and descending, had a great dive and saw lots of coral and different types of fish including Lion fish, Moray Eels, Scorpion fish and a blue spiky starfish.

Back on shore we rinsed the kit and went and chatted to some other couples before grabbing some yummy local food at a place down the beach called Limbo.

The next morning was pretty chilled, did some reading and got our gear ready for our second dive, this time we went out on a slightly choppier sea to a site called Arus Balee which we were informed means widows current, nice! The dive was around a rock and there was lots to see, we could feel the current a bit but it wasn’t too bad. We had a few problems with our masks fogging up due to us forgetting to clean them beforehand so we were kind of struggling to see but we got through it. Saw a lot of the same fish as the day before.

We headed back to shore and sat on the balcony as the weather turned stormy, we had a bit of rain which was nice as it cooled the air.

Went back to our local, Limbo for food then watched Juno in our room. What a great film!

We started our final day with a breakfast of banana pancakes which were fab then went for a short snorkel before going out for our third dive.

This time it was a wreck dive which we hadn’t done before so we were pretty excited, we sped over to Sabang bay and only had to descend about 15m till we could see the old tug boat. There was an impressive amount of fish life on the boat and around it was an amazing coral garden which housed many of the fish we had already seen but also some tiny pipefish, lots more reef fish including clown fish living in their anemone homes. We also saw an octopus trying to catch a fish!

We ascended back to the boat and went to a different dive site where we got to see hot springs coming up through the sand. This was a great experience seeing the water bubbling up through the ocean floor and everyone enjoyed relaxing in the warm patches knowing where they came from!

After getting back to shore we swapped tanks and had a bit of a rest before going for a beach dive with one of the guides Tim. The house reef was possibly the best dive that we did, it was really relaxed and all our kit was working fine for us. It wasn’t too deep so we had lots of air and we saw so much that every couple of minutes Tim would be pointing something else out to us. We saw lots of Lion fish and Scorpion fish some of which were pretty big, saw a couple of Stingrays, lots of reef fish, an angler fish, Moray Eels, Octopus, and the list goes on.

That evening we joined the team for some beers to say goodbye to a guy who had been there for a few months, it seems to be the type of place that if you are not careful you could end up spending a few months or years of your life there rather than a few days, not that that would be a bad thing but it sounded to us like that was the story for most of the people there.

Stayed up pretty late with the guys and gals of LL drinking beers and eating nasi goreng, during the evening we were being persuaded to stay by a couple of people there and we were thinking about it but by the morning in the sober light of day we realised that we had to move on to see the rest of Indonesia. We had a final breakfast at our favourite eating place and arranged for one of the local guys to take us back to the port in his car, we would have gone back in a becak but didn’t know how to get one from where we were.

Saw quite a few small monkeys on our way to the port and more cows!

Ferry ride back to Banda Aceh was a bit rougher and longer than the way over so we slept for most of it, when we got off the boat our new friend Adlias was waiting for us to take us to the bus station where we would be getting the night bus to Medan.

Adlias helped us to buy our bus tickets then we said goodbye and waited around in the bus station, we wanted to get some food and stalls were setting up ready for the feasting to begin but we felt bad getting food whilst everyone waited for sundown so we ended up buying some packs of biscuits and water to keep us going on the bus.

We were getting a bit impatient waiting for the bus as we couldn’t get an answer from the guy behind the desk as to when the bus would be arriving, even using our best Indonesian! Luckily soon after that the bus swung into the station and we were ushered on. Then the ‘apparently’ one-stop bus stopped numerous times in the first twenty minutes, this wasn’t a bad thing as I used one instance to get off and grab more food!

The rest of the thirteen hour bus journey was definitely an experience and consisted of a couple of lovely Indonesian Muslim ladies helping us to recline our seats and offering us food, random Indonesian music, more stops to pick people up or let them off the bus, constant beeping of the horn from the driver and others, they seem to use this method to let each other know they are there! Lots of erratic driving which eventually we ignored and managed to get a couple of hours broken sleep and amazingly we only saw one accident which was a small overturned truck.

There was a long stop at about three in the morning for everyone to get off and eat before their fasting would begin for the next day, we couldn’t face eating at that time so stayed on the bus.

By morning we had arrived in the metropolis of Medan and were instantly engulfed in a swarm of cars, bikes and becaks. The Lonely Planet says that Medan usually ends up top of the ‘worst place you have visited’ list so we will have to see for ourselves, it’s got one night to prove us wrong!
