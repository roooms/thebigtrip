---
title: "Whistler week two"
date: 2010-03-20 00:14:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
Week two (and a bit of week three) in Whistler saw a few more great powder days, the beginning of the 2010 Paralympic Games, our first night ice skating and Jon and Jenna from Seattle joining us on the slopes.

We started the week with some free expert tuition from one of the guys staying in our chalet. Lots of riding switch and finding little jumps at the side of the runs. The forecast suggested it would be snowing heavily over the next few days so we were looking forward to heading out with the locals and finding secret deep powder stashes.

The following day we opened the blinds in our room and it was like someone had stolen Whistler Blackcomb. We can usually see both mountains from our balcony, but it was just white. Undeterred we thought it might be clear at the top and decided to try out the longest run on the mountain, from the peak of Whistler to the creek at its base. We could check out the Paralympic downhill course at the same time so it seemed like a good idea.

Arriving at the top it felt like the eye of the storm. 20ft visibility and the highest winds we had experienced while snowboarding, left us unable to see what was mountain and what was sky. We very carefully made our way down the peak-to-creek run, keeping a look out for the piste markers at the edge of the run. This, the longest run, had every type of weather and snow condition going, zero-vis, perfect blue skies with incredible powder, ice, then slush at the bottom. It was like a weeks snowboarding in one go.

On Friday we headed out in our biggest group yet (nine), some of which had been here since the season opened in November 2009. We were treated to some great untracked tree runs first thing, only to be a bit disappointed when one lift we needed to catch was closed due to such heavy snow fall. We stayed in the same area on Blackcomb all day and could run fresh lines until the last lift, but we were also surprised at just how quickly off-piste gets tracked here in Whistler compared to Europe. We finished the day off with $4 ice skating and joined in an ice hockey free-for-all at one end of the rink.

Saturday was supposed to be the big powder day but the snow eased off Friday night, so I took the opportunity to demo a [different board](http://lib-tech.com/snow/snowboards/phoenix-series-jamie-lynn/) in what powder was left. It was great fun riding a 'banana' board for the day and I'm looking forward to testing it again before we leave. We got off the slopes a bit earlier as (becoming snow snobs) the crunchy remains of powder were much less fun than the day before.

On Sunday, Jon and Jenna arrived in the evening and we met them for a bite to eat and some beers. Lots of beer, baileys and whisky later, we got to bed around 4am, looking forward to being up bright and early for the next days riding. The snow was much better on Monday and we got to ride some amazing double black diamond runs including Couloir Extreme and Spanky's Ladder. Spanky's was possibly the steepest run I have ridden and it was a real buzz, opening up in to a massive powder bowl with only three or four other people in the same area of the mountain. We headed out for more drinks that evening and were looking forward to the Paralympic Giant Slalom the next day.

Tuesday greeted us with lots of rain. We caught the bus to Creekside, grabbed Starbucks and had our first chairlift ride dressed in normal clothing without snowboards strapped to our feet. It took us up about 800 metres to the base of the downhill run, where we got to watch some amazing athletes completing their first runs of the day. The visually impaired skiers had guides skiing ten to twenty feet in front of them, some with speakers strapped to their backs so the athlete could hear where they were. The commentators would announce when a totally blind athlete was competing and the whole crowd had to stay silent until he or she had crossed the finish line. Watching the event and cheering everyone on, it was inspirational to see the determination of those taking part.

After the first runs of the visually impaired and [sit-skiers](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoski) were complete, we headed home to change out of our now completely drenched clothing, in to our snowboarding gear so we could go back and watch the second (medal event) runs. There were some British athletes and a great group of British supporters, and after watching the last run of the day, we caught the bus home, surprisingly tired for a non-snowboarding day.

Wednesday was a bit of a planning day (and St Patrick's Day!) and we sat down to work out where we are going in Canada once we leave Whistler, and what we will do and see in the US before leaving via Los Angeles at the beginning of May. We met up with some of the guys that had been out riding for some early evening après at a bar called Crystal Lounge and joined in the party with the leprechauns.

At some point during our stay (possibly whilst under the influence of alcohol), I agreed to switch my board for a pair of ski's for a day, with a guy in our chalet that had the same size feet. That day had arrived and we had gained a complete beginner snowboarder to make things even more interesting. Nat would be teaching Laetitia, the groups beginner, I was under the instruction of Will (the guy back-flipping the kicker in the last bunch of photos), and James (using my board) had Toby (the kicker build co-ordinator) showing him the ropes.

It was a great day, just because we were all so bad to start with and were being overtaken by four-year-old speed demons whilst trying not to fall over. By the end of the day James and I had both completed a blue run in our new found speciality, and Laetitia had determined her brand new board badly needed waxing before her first 'official' ski school lesson the following day.

That brings us up to today, and we spent the whole day in the [terrain park](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrain_park). The forecast was predicting a few days of great sunny weather, but no new snow. We practiced jumps and boxes, and a few 180's before grabbing some beers ready for a BBQ back at the chalet.
