Our train to Agra was another late one, it arrived in Agra five hours late which was frustrating as we had wanted to go and see the Taj Mahal in the morning. Never mind, we managed to find an auto-rickshaw driver and persuaded him to take us to hotel Siddhartha (rather than his choice) which we had been recommended by Mark and Sam.

The guy there was very friendly and loved doing his cockney accent, constantly asking us if we were ‘alright?’

We took a room and welcomed the buckets of hot water to make ourselves respectable for our visit to the Taj. The first glimpse we got was from the rooftop of the guesthouse, it took my breath away especially as I wasn’t expecting to be able to see it that well from there!

After some food we went to buy our entry tickets which cost seventy-five times the price of an Indian entrance ticket!

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2011/01/20267194-P1110934.JPG)

A tiny bit of history just in case you didn’t know. The Taj Mahal was built by Emperor Shah Jahan as a memorial to his second wife, Mumtaz Mahal, who died giving birth to their 14th child in 1631. Construction began that year and the building was completed in 1653, not long after it was finished the Emperor was overthrown by his son Aurangzeb and imprisoned in Agra Fort where he could only gaze at his creation from afar until his death in 1666.

We entered the complex through the west gate and walked through a second gate so that we came face to face with that famous view of the Taj. It’s white marble was gleaming in the afternoon sun and we decided that any time must be a good time to take in this masterpiece, it was busy but that didn’t take anything away from the atmosphere.

After taking a load of pictures we started walking towards it, stopping every now and again to take in where we were. Once we reached the steps up to the Taj we had to put some very attractive protective shoe covers on to protect the marble. We walked around the outside admiring the intricate work and colourful inlays. We then entered the Taj and saw the old tombs where Emperor Shah Jahan and his wife Mumtaz Mahal were once buried. The real tombs are locked in a basement room.

Back outside in the warm sun we took a break for a bit and just relaxed in the buildings presence before walking through the gardens and back to the place where we had entered. The Taj now looked a bit more golden in the late afternoon sun and I could see how it changes colour during the day.

Definitely one of the best places we have been to on our trip!

The rest of our afternoon was taken up with collecting one of our train tickets from a different guesthouse and then going for some tasty food at a rooftop restaurant which had a view of the Taj but unfortunately at night it was not so good.

We didn't really have much time to see Agra but it seemed another chaotic place mainly there for the tourists, the Fort looked pretty impressive but we just didn't have to time to see anything else.

After a good nights sleep we were up and heading back to the train station to catch the next leg of our trip to Jaipur, this was late too so we spent time playing cards and drinking chai till our train arrived. It was a nice journey and I managed to get some pictures out of the open window when the train slowed down for stations. It was also nice to be warm on a train journey for once.
