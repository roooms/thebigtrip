After a chilly nights sleep we layered up before heading out to find the Golden Temple. The streets were buzzing with activity but it had a different feel to Delhi, we found the tourist info place outside the entrance to the temple and got directions to the sleeping quarters. While we were there a policeman gently persuaded us to book for the border closing ceremony later in the afternoon, this is an elaborate ceremony which takes place everyday at the India/Pakistan border.

We were glad to have a bit of a plan for our day and headed off to find our accommodation for the night. As we found the block we also got our first view of the Golden Temple, it looked amazing and was glowing against the white walls surrounding it.

A guy showed us to the dorm rooms and we were able to have a room that sleeps three all to ourselves, it felt instantly warmer than the room last night and even had hot water. Not bad for a place which you can stay in for free or for a donation!

After locking our stuff away we headed out to a little food place I had spied on the way to the Temple, the men there welcomed us in and we settled into one of the tastiest meals we have had in a while along with some proper chai. The guys came to chat to us including an elderly man who had brilliant English, he was explaining that although the Golden Temple is a Sikh holy place anyone is welcome to go there and pray or just be and enjoy. We certainly felt welcomed in and were really enjoying the slightly more relaxed pace of this Punjab community, as we left we already decided where we would be having our breakfast the next day.

Then it was time to see the Temple, we had to remove our shoes and socks which was a bit of a struggle due to the temperature but were pleasantly surprised by the warm water hand wash sinks and foot baths, after covering our heads we were allowed into the Temple. It really is an amazing, calm and spiritual place. The Temple sits in the middle of a sacred pool where people were stripping down to their Lungis and going for a dip in the non-heated water. There were people meditating along the marble walkways and everyone seemed happy and smiling and wanted to chat and of course take photos!

After deciding not to go for a swim in the pool of nectar we dipped our feet into the water which is said to heal the body and walked around to the causeway which leads to the Temple. The queue was quite big but as we stood there the sun came out to warm us up a little bit.

Once inside the Temple we saw the four priests who chant all day long, this is broadcast around the complex by loudspeakers. The Golden Temple is the day resting place for the Sikh holy book called the Guru Granth Sahib which is ceremoniously brought out in the morning and put to bed at night in a different but still beautiful building. We walked around inside listening to the rhythmic chanting and enjoying the feeling of thick carpet under our bare feet before heading back outside to collect our shoes. Due to the lack of entrance fee we decided to go back later to see the evening ceremony.

We had time for a bit of a wander through the chaotic streets and a mini mug of chai with some rickshaw drivers before it was time to get our share taxi to the border which ended up being a miniature mini van and was filled with six Indian adults, two kids and us. The journey was ok and while Dan had a power nap I watched Indian daily life going on through the window.

Once at the border we walked past loads of popcorn sellers and headed towards the border, we could hear music playing and lots of cheering and applauding already. After going through security in our separate male/female queues we were able to get VIP privileges as we were foreigners and this got us a bit closer to the action.

There were loads of people packed into an amphitheatre being whipped into a frenzy by a compare with a mic. There was the same set up on the Pakistani side but there didn’t seem to be as many people. The Indian and what we could see of the Pakistani Army outfits were pretty special and just as we were observing how tall they all were the ceremony began with some loud yelling and two female soldiers fast marching down to the gate. The whole ceremony consisted of lots of yelling, very expressive marching, impressive high-kicking and pantomime worthy stamping. At the end though both sides lower their flags at the same time to show respect and then the gates are closed and it is all over for another day We left with the masses and I managed to get a photo with one of the Indian Army guys, we were also mobbed for photos on our way back to the van.

Back at the temple we decided to check out the free/donation community dining room (Guru-Ka-Langar). This is a feature of Sikh temples and is designed to show unity among people. The kitchens cater for up to 40,000 pilgrims a day. We collected our thali tray, bowl and spoon and followed everyone upstairs to a huge room with seating mats on the floor. Grabbed a space and before we knew it we were being served water, two chapattis, dhal, bean curry and the most amazing coconut rice pudding You could even have seconds if you wanted. We took our empty plates downstairs and saw where the magic happens, huge vats of curry being stirred, an army of people sat around peeling garlic and the biggest organisation of dish washing I have ever seen.

Feeling full we headed off to see the temple at night, the feet were feeling even more chilly but the sight was well worth it. We went and sat inside the temple and listened to the chanting for a while. Just before the ceremony we headed out and waited to catch some pics, we soon saw a bed type contraption being carried to the Golden Temple and not long after it returned carrying the Guru (book).

It was time to go and warm up and just as we were discussing getting a hot drink a guy told us that there was chai in the kitchen. We followed him in and had two big bowls of steaming chai followed by more photos with him and his mates.

Back in the room after witnessing loads of people huddled together sleeping outside in the courtyard area we felt very fortunate to have our cosy little dorm room with its quilted blankets and chatted about what an amazing experience the Golden Temple has been.

After a much warmer night’s sleep we got up and chatted to some of the other people staying in the dorms, one of them was an American Sikh called Surab Sarong We all watched him put on his turban before he did our numerology.

We went back to the same place as yesterday for food and it was just as good as the day before, the old guy looked at our palms and said we will both be very lucky in our lives and they hoped to see us again one day.

We went deeper into the small streets today and ended up at Katra Jaimal Singh Bazaar where the streets are filled with beautiful saris, scarves and material. We also visited Jallianwala Bagh which is a memorial park commemorating 2000 Indians killed or wounded by the British back in 1919. Even though the place had a sad history it was nice to walk around somewhere away from all the walla’s and touts. We didn’t escape the photo requests and stares though.

After packing up our bags we went for a final meal in the kitchen before getting an auto-rickshaw to the train station. As we arrived we spotted one of the guys from the dorms and he said that the train was running late, what a surprise!

We joined him and his travel buddy Evan to wander round some shops to waste a bit of time then when we returned to the station we were very happy to find some of the famous bourbon biscuits that helped Mark and Sam through their long train journeys.

After yet another chai on the platform we went to check on the train and were relieved to see that it was arriving soon. We wandered over to the platform to find it waiting there so boarded and found our beds for the next twenty four hours.

It was a chilly night on the train but luckily we woke to the chai-walla walking the isle and enjoyed two consecutive cups, they are only small servings after all. Had a tasty curry and roti then spent the day reading, sleeping, snacking, drinking chai and watching India race past the window.

We finally arrived in Varanasi at 12:45am, met Steve and Evan off the train and joined forces to battle the auto-rickshaw drivers and find a decent place to crash for the night.

The driver was a good guy and delivered us safely through the dense fog to the Elvis guesthouse where we found cheap rooms to get a good nights sleep after our epic train journey.
