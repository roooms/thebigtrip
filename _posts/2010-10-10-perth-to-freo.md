We were really excited to be back in Australia. Indonesia had been an incredible experience, but we were looking forward to a break from the hassling and haggling. After collecting our bags we grabbed a coffee and a pie - something I had surprisingly missed since we were last in Australia - and had a flick through a leaflet listing accommodation in Perth.

We had to take a shuttle bus from the airport to the city centre, and Brendan our driver told us we should forget about the places we had looked up and instead go to a place called Underground Backpackers in the Northbridge area of Perth. On the way in to the centre of Perth we couldn’t quite believe just how pretty and well maintained everything was. All the grass looked like it was part of a golf course fairway. There was no litter or graffiti and the buildings looked like they had just been cleaned. Brendan phoned ahead while we were on the bus and got us in a dorm room, and on our arrival we were greeted with a huge building that had a bar, cinema room and swimming pool. We got a free drink voucher on check-in and after dropping our bags in the room, went down to the bar to see what was going on.

It was a Friday afternoon and the place was full of Irish labourers who had no doubt spent the week working hard and now wanted to blow as much cash as possible. We got stuck chatting to people and as the evening drew on we realized we wouldn’t get out to see Perth on our first night.

The next morning we got up late, nursing slightly fuzzy heads and went for a walk in to the heart of Perth. We had a quick look around the main shopping district, which was alive with people milling around, before heading down to the edge of the Swan River and relaxing on the grass. On the way back to the hostel we grabbed some food for the next few days, and then crashed out in our room and watched films on the netbook, hoping tomorrow we would have more energy to see what Perth had to offer.

On our second full day we surfaced earlier and hopped on to one of the free buses that stops just a block from the entrance to Kings Park - the biggest inner-city park in the world. It is to the west of Perth and elevated high enough to provide a great view back over the city centre. It was a busy Sunday in the park as there was a festival on with live music and kids shows. We spent a few hours wandering through the botanical gardens, checking out the native plants and flowers, before relaxing with a bite to eat while listening to the live music.

We walked back to the hostel, giving us chance to see the west side of the city. It turned out to be a bit soulless and was mostly wholesale outlets and warehouses, but it gave us the opportunity to check bus and train timetables at the interchange for our hop to Cottesloe.

Leaving the hostel the next day we made our way to the train station and caught the train to Cottesloe - a small residential area west of Perth, overlooking the Indian Ocean. It was picture perfect Australian suburbs, with big driveways leading up to detached houses. We walked from the tiny station along wide roads surrounded by grass and parks, until we arrived at the waters edge, then followed it south to the Ocean Beach Backpackers. The rest of the afternoon was spent soaking up the sun on the beach just across the road from the hostel.

The next day we walked further south to the main Cottesloe Beach area and soaked up some more rays. The beach and surf was busy with people chilling out or surfing. We came to the conclusion that a large percentage of Cottesloe’s residents no longer need to work and get to spend their days enjoying themselves at the beach or the nearby golf course. There were a handful of shops and cafés at Cottesloe Beach but on the whole it was a sleepy place to enjoy the sea, sand and amazing west coast sunset.

We decided we could see Fremantle (aka Freo) in a day rather than staying a night there as well, so caught a train from Cottesloe south to the end of the line and spent the day wandering the small town full of Victorian buildings and history museums. We checked out a free exhibition at Fremantle Prison and bought some new thongs after I broke my last pair on the way out of the hostel in Perth; then made our way down to the fishing wharf and fought off the seagulls to enjoy fish and chips, with the added ambience of the salty air and fishing boats squeaking against their moorings.

Happy we had seen the best of Freo we caught the train back for our last evening in Cottesloe before we would return to Perth. We planned to find a Greyhound office the next day and buy tickets to get us up the west coast of Australia to Broome.

Back in Perth we managed to get a four-bed dorm room in Underground Backpackers, but it was much smaller and more cramped than on our previous stay and we were glad it would just be for the night. We walked in to town and found a travel agent to get more information on the Greyhound tickets and what we could see on the way up the coast. It was here that we found out the Greyhounds don’t really stop at any of the sights on the west coast. They stick to the highway and do drop-offs at roadhouses every three-or-four-hundred kilometres where you need to book extra transport to take you to places like Monkey Mia or Coral Bay. We added up the cost of the greyhound tickets, shuttle buses to each destination, accommodation and food costs and realised it wouldn’t cost much more to go on an organised tour between Perth and Broome. The added bonus was that we would see Karijini National Park, somewhere we would have needed to hire a jeep if we were to see on our own. We booked on the tour and at the same time bought flights from Broome to Darwin and then on to Cairns.

We had been worrying quite a bit about how to navigate the sparsely populated west coast of Australia, and now could breathe a slight sigh of relief as we had organised the next three weeks and would end up on the east coast and in familiar territory.
