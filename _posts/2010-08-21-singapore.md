We had time in the morning to grab an awesome breakfast with Sammy and Giles before heading to the airport. Next stop Singapore!

We had booked a hotel in the Little India district of Singapore, and managed to find it without any hassle. Our tiny box room was suffering pretty badly from damp and the bathroom floor never seemed to dry, but it was in a cool area so we were happy to get it.

Nat’s mum and dad would be arriving only a few hours later, so we had time for a wander around the Little India area before making our way to their hotel. It was a nice feeling being back somewhere I considered so familiar yet so different. As always, the place was buzzing with activity. We met up with Colin and Jude at their hotel and then walked back toward Little India to show them around, but it was getting late and most of the places we’d walked passed before were now shut. We had to settle for a few beers in their hotel and planned to meet up the next morning.

We had breakfast before taking in the tourist attractions Singapore has to offer. There were quite a few new ones since my last proper look around in 2006. The biggest attention seeker must be the Marina Bay Sands hotel that has been built on a large section of reclaimed land at the marina. We followed the river and stopped at Boat Quay for a few drinks while a brief rain shower moved through. We checked out the Raffles Hotel Long Bar then caught the Youth Olympic Games Opening Ceremony, before chilling out at a food court and people watching for the rest of the evening. With aching feet we hobbled back to the hotel.

The next morning we ate at the same food court before catching a bus to the Botanical Gardens. It was great to escape all the high-rise for a few hours and wander through greenery. Back in Little India we tried to find a restaurant that Nat and I had eaten in last time we were here but just missed it and ate a few doors down. We only realised when we emerged completely stuffed back on to the street and walked straight passed it. We took a look around the Kampong Glam area of Little India, before heading up to the New Asia Bar on the 71st floor of Raffles City, to show Colin and Jude the view. It was still just as cool as I remember, and free!

Two days in and we found a great Indian place to have breakfast. Chapatti’s, Thosai or Prata, it all tasted great. We made our way over to Singapore’s Island Resort - Sentosa - and saw the huge changes that have taken place. Universal Studios! A roller-coaster! We walked along the man-made beach and saw all the new hotels that have been built overlooking the water. The only downside is it’s still surrounded by loads of cargo ships waiting to dock at the port.

Our last full day started at the same Indian Restaurant for breakfast, before Nat went shopping with her parents and I dropped in on the WDS Singapore office to see the guys. We met up again to visit the Sky Park at Marina Bay Sands, two hundred metres above the water. The hotel has an amazing infinity pool and lounge area on what looks like a ships deck high above the marina. As guests of the park but not the hotel, we weren’t able to go in the pool, but it was definitely worth a look. While we were up there the heavens opened and we were treated to an amazing rain storm. The rest of Singapore disappeared in to a thick grey fog for ten to fifteen minutes. That evening we visited Maxwell Food Court and tried Durian for the first (and last time) before taking a quick dip in the hotel pool where Jude and Colin were staying.

On our last morning in Singapore we got breakfast and picked up snacks ready for our eight hour train journey to Kuala Lumpur. We had to stop briefly at the border and get off the train to go through customs, but other than that we just got to sit back and take in the rapidly changing scenery as we entered Malaysia.
