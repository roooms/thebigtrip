---
title: "Death Valley & Las Vegas"
date: 2010-05-02 01:18:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
The drive to Barstow was uneventful and on arrival we were surrounded by motels. Staying the night in an Econolodge we checked over our route for the next day and settled on two possible hotels for Las Vegas. We also spent more time looking in to the Cook Islands, our next stop once we leave the USA.

The next morning we slapped on the sunscreen and drove north to Shoshone, which consisted of a petrol station, a very small museum and a cafe where we grabbed a quick lemonade in a jar, before heading in to Death Valley.

We had read that for a few weeks in April, Death Valley changes from a barren landscape to one covered in yellow and purple flowers. We were lucky enough to be here at that time and stopped every so often at points of interest on the way. It was blisteringly hot out of the car and we were surprised to see there was a golf course in the middle of the valley.

As we left the park we had another two and a half hours drive on to Las Vegas, Nevada. Once we had signal back on the phone we called both the hotels we were thinking of booking in to and got prices. We were surprised at how cheap accommodation was in Vegas, but I guess it's that there is so much competition. We settled on the Stratosphere on Las Vegas Boulevard, the main strip.

We didn't book the hotel over the phone and managed to get an even better rate by just arriving and paying for a room at the front desk. We got changed and headed to the 109th floor observation deck of the Stratosphere tower as it was included free in our room booking.

There were [four rides on top of the tower](http://www.stratospherehotel.com/thrills/), two of which hung you over the edge so you could see eight hundred feet down to the street below, and one which allowed you to jump from the edge and, attached to a "descender" cable, fall down to a third floor landing mat. It was VERY tempting but as they cost extra I decided to stick to the original plan and save myself for one of the worlds highest bungee jumps in New Zealand.

One of the staff offered to give us a run down of the strip and what to see and what could be missed. She was from Singapore and explained that it was "easy to get screwed over" in Vegas as a foreigner so she always liked to help visitors from outside the USA. With an annotated map in hand we eventually found our way out of the casino within the Stratosphere and on to the strip.

Each hotel has a free show it puts on at certain times of day. We saw a [volcano erupt outside The Mirage](http://www.mirage.com/attractions/volcano.aspx) and a huge [choreographed fountain display to music at Bellagio](http://www.bellagio.com/amenities/fountains-of-bellagio.aspx). Then we got lost inside Caesars Palace shopping mall for an hour. It's so big the security guards ride around on Segways. We made it about half-way down the strip before we were ready to turn back and see the rest the next day.

The next morning we grabbed a bus pass each and decided to head to the other end of the strip and work our way back. It took 45 minutes on the bus to travel the 4 miles that is the main street (Las Vegas Boulevard) from our hotel to Mandalay Bay. We then found out the Mandalay Bay was closed for a private party. Oh well, working our way back towards home we stopped at New York New York to ride the roller coaster and MGM Grand to see their Lion Habitat. We also managed to get some discounted tickets to see Cirque du Soleil's Mystère show at Treasure Island that evening.

We got back to our hotel and dipped our toes in the outdoor pool on the eighth floor to cool them off from all the walking, then had to make our way back to Treasure Island to watch the show. We had planned to see a Cirque show in London with friends a while ago but it never happened. We then nearly saw their latest show called KOOZA in Portland, Oregon when we saw them performing in a square there. Deciding to put it off until Vegas was now proving to be a great decision. Mystère was the original Cirque du Soleil show and it was incredible to watch the cast perform. I can't really put it in to words so recommend [watching their trailer](http://www.cirquedusoleil.com/en/shows/mystere/media/official-video.aspx).

We left the next day and drove the two hundred and seventy five miles to the Grand Canyon.
