---
title: "Medan"
date: 2010-09-04 04:19:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
As soon as we were off the night bus we were surrounded by drivers and agents for taxis, minibuses and becaks all wanting to know where we were going. We got our bags in to a cheap becak and headed for Jalan SM Raja, the ‘backpacker’ street of Medan.

We checked in at the Pondok Wisata Angel and got a good price for an air-con room before settling down for some breakfast at their quaint ground-floor restaurant/café. Unfortunately it was open-fronted on to one of the busiest roads in Medan, but it sort of added to the atmosphere and we watched the world race past as we enjoyed a bite to eat.

We took a walk up and down SM Raja to see what it had to offer. The was a mosque further up on the left and a road that had been closed for Ramadan and was covered in stalls and seating for the feast after dusk tonight. We picked up some supplies and looked in to how we would get to Bukit Lawang the following day. All private transport was cheap in Pounds Sterling, but we were getting used to the Indonesian Rupiah now and planned on getting the public bus the hundred kilometres to Bukit Lawang for less than a pound each.

Back at our room we caught up on a few hours sleep having had such a restless journey during the night. As dusk was closing in we had a colder shower each in the smallest bathroom I’ve ever seen outside of a campervan, slapped on some mozzie repellent and went for another walk.

We took some pictures of the two main mosques in the area we were staying, located at each end of the street hosting the Ramadan fair, and decided we should get some food from one of the local stalls rather than back at the Angel. We were invited to every table outside various tiny stalls as people tried to get you to try their food. In the end we settled on one purely because they had a menu we could read. We were surrounded by young teenagers working at the stalls who all came over to see what we would eat and one girl asked if she could join us to practice her English. We had a great time chatting to them all and learned some new Indonesian phrases at the same time.

Returning to the air-conditioned room was incredible after the heat outside, but unfortunately the window didn’t really fit in the hole in the wall where it had been placed so the road noise was the same with it open or closed. Ear plugs in, I was out like a light.

Waking up the next morning it was surreal to think it was my birthday. We had a few things we needed to do, so we got up reasonably early and had breakfast before going to the cake shop opposite and buying a birthday doughnut. A ring doughnut with chocolate icing and chocolate sprinkles I’m sure you will want to know. From there we got a becak to Sriwajaya Air on the next street to see if we could book flights to Jakarta for September 1st, the day we return from Bukit Lawang. They even managed to beat their own internet price.

Arriving at the bus station the price of our journey to Bukit Lawang started out at over three times what we wanted to pay, but it took less than a minute for them to come down to our figure. The LP guide has been really helpful in that respect, especially as it’s the latest edition (this years) so the quoted prices are as accurate as the descriptions.

On the bus I quickly devoured the doughnut that had been waiting patiently in my rucksack as we ticked off our to-do list. We had to wait for a while for the bus to fill up, but soon enough we were on our way to what we hoped would be a beautiful village in the jungle, bordering one of only two national parks in the world where you can still see the Orang-utan in the wild. There was also a restaurant that served good pizza, which seemed like another luxury we could treat ourselves to since it was my birthday!
