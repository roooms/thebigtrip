---
title: "Wanaka & Treble Cone"
date: 2010-07-16 00:14:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
The alarm went off at 6:30am. Still dark outside, it would have been difficult to muster the enthusiasm to get up if it were any day other than the first of five days snowboarding at Treble Cone, a reasonable sized resort just under thirty kilometres from Wanaka. The reason for the very early start was a guy on the camp site had offered to give us a lift up to the mountain, but was leaving at an inconceivable hour as his son was starting race training and had to be there before 8am. We had decided this would be a great opportunity to meet someone who knew the area well and avoid driving the campervan up the steep, winding track that accesses the base of the ski slopes. Jason, our chauffeur for the day, would turn out to be one of the nicest people we’d met on our travels so far. Along with two other guys, Hutch and Steve, we clambered in to a 4x4 and the six of us set off for Treble Cone.

We got there before the lifts opened but it gave us chance to grab a coffee and buy our lift passes, then spent the morning being guided around the slopes. It was great to be back on the boards so quickly having jumped across the equator in to the southern hemispheres winter. It had been just ninety-five days since we unclipped our bindings for the last time in Whistler. The snow coverage wasn’t brilliant though so we were restricted to staying on-piste most of the time. We weren’t expecting any fresh stuff either so we knew the first few days would be the best we would get.

After two days of boarding we were surprisingly tired, and as our pass allowed us days off in between we decided to spend an extra day in Wanaka just having a better look around. We checked out the shops and drooled over all the new seasons gear, and decided to go and see what was showing at the cinema. Cinema Paradiso has a bit of a reputation and draws a big crowd considering it’s small single screen, showing the latest releases but also older classics. This is most likely due to the excellent pizza and delicious freshly baked cookies that you can buy during the intermission. Yup, they stop the film half way through, and everyone goes to the counter to get fresh snacks and drinks. Combined with that genius idea, the cinema room is full of old sofas, lazy-boys, even airplane seats and an old Morris Minor you can sit in and watch the film. We grabbed a sofa and watched some kids fill the car with seat cushions so they were as comfortable as possible.

We boarded for the next three days and had some ups and downs. The slopes were begging for more snow but none came. The snow cannons were running most of the time to add to the thin base, but it wasn’t enough to improve the conditions that much. From speaking to skiers and boarders who were trying out other hills we were happy with our choice though. Cardrona, another resort between Wanaka and Queenstown had slightly better snow, but was packed with kids on their school holiday as it has a greater selection of beginner slopes. Treble Cone had escaped the masses and that made the lift queues much more bearable.

On our last night in Wanaka we were planning a return visit to the cinema. I had decided if I had to watch Sex and the City 2 somewhere with Nat, this was most likely the best cinema in the world to do so. I was already imagining biting in to a white chocolate and ginger cookie during the break when a better opportunity presented itself. Another couple on the camp site who we’d chilled out with most evenings in the kitchen/lounge had no plans and chatting in the spa it emerged Kerry wanted to see Sex and the City 2 as well. And so it was that the girls disappeared off to the cinema via a bar in town just as the All Blacks began demolishing the Springboks in the Tri-Nations. To top it all off Nat brought me back a cookie!
