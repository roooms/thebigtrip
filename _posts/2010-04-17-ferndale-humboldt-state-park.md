---
title: "Ferndale & Humboldt State Park"
date: 2010-04-17 23:45:00
author: "Dan Brown"
---
There weren't going to be any chain motels in Ferndale, the community wouldn't allow it. It was a must see destination on our way down the coast though, so we headed for the cheapest lodgings we knew of and got a room for the night. Every house was perfectly maintained and of a different time.Victorian style shop fronts lined the main street. We checked in before heading to the coast about 5 miles west of the town.

The surf was a bit crazy, but there were two or three people in the water catching waves. We wandered a mile or so down the shore as Nat took millions of pictures of the sunset. A guy had brought his parrot to the beach for the evening and was laying on the sand as it walked around him, one of the weirder things I have seen.

Driving back in to town we knew everything closed early, but thought a walk around before the light was completely gone would help us get a better feel for the area. The butchers shop made sandwiches so we would come back for them tomorrow.

In the morning we checked out and loaded the car, had one last look around, and went to get our sandwiches. In the butchers there were huge mounted animal heads on the walls above us. We looked for somewhere nice to sit and eat our brunch, but before we knew it had made it back to the car without finding anywhere. Next stop was Avenue of the Giants.

There is twenty or so mile stretch of road that splits from highway 101 south of Ferndale and twists its way through the coast redwoods of Humboldt State Park. It's been named Avenue of the Giants, and being surrounded by trees over twelve feet thick and three hundred feet tall makes you feel kind of small. It also gives you neck ache pretty quickly.

We stopped of at Founders Grove as there was a documented, self-guided tour available. Walking around the trail we did some tree hugging and took some pictures stood next to the root systems of fallen redwoods. We then headed to an area called Rockefeller Forest and wandered down to a creek running through the trees. The sun was out and it was tempting to go in for a swim, but a quick check of the water temperature put paid to that idea.

At the south end of the Avenue of Giants is the Big Tree Drive-Thru where we tried our best not to scratch the rental car as we crept through a 7ft wide gap in a redwood. Very 'touristy' but it had to be done!

That was our day in the trees, and once we were out on to the open road, Route 101 soon became the beautiful but incredibly twisty Highway 1, and we were right back on the California coast. Next stop Mendocino.
