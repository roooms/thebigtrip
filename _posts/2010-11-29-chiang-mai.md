We hadn’t booked any accommodation in Chiang Mai but had been tipped off by Katie, who we met in Western Australia, that SK2 was the place to stay. Run by a very friendly guy called Nick, when we arrived he was surprised we hadn’t booked ahead as the Loi Krathong festival (also known as Yi Peng) was this weekend and Chang Mai would be full to bursting with foreigners and Thais for three days. Luckily he had one room free and after a bit of negotiating we agreed on a reasonable 400 baht per night, about £8. SK2 was more a hotel than a hostel, with a swimming pool on the ground floor and a sun terrace on the roof. It had a great restaurant and a tour and activity booking desk for the many things you could entertain yourself with in and around Chiang Mai. It was within the crumbling walls of the old city, a mile-square area also bound by a moat with various crossing points linking it to the sprawl outside.

On our first evening we walked down to one of the main roads which was lined with bars, restaurants and a surprising amount of book shops. There we also coffee houses and tour services, and even a 7-Eleven. We grabbed a couple of beers from a bar called Yellow Zoe, before tucking in to Pad Thai, cooked while we watched by a guy at the side of the road, with a little area of plastic tables and chairs. The food was delicious and as we paid up we practised our first Wai in Thailand.

**Friday 19th November**

In the morning we walked back down to the same street for breakfast. I wanted to try a hopf coffee but due to a poor harvest the coffee house didn‘t have any. I settled for an Americano, while Nat had an OJ. Next stop was for some food and after a wander up to the main road surrounding the old city of Chiang Mai, we found a little café that did great boiled eggs. The rest of the day was spent walking around the many temples within the old city walls.

That evening we went back out to watch the monks at Wat Phra Singh before heading to a little restaurant called The Corner where I had a green curry and Nat a red. The night market was only a short walk from the restaurant so we headed there to see what we could find. I picked up a pair of cotton trousers for £2 and Nat had her eye on an elephant charm for her bracelet. She decided to see what else was around as we could always come back another time.

**Saturday 20th November**

We were up early and really excited to be going to see elephants at a family run park about an hour from Chiang Mai. We had time for breakfast before our driver picked us up. Warawut, or ‘Wood’ for short was not just the driver, but also the owner of the park his family owned and ran. He had four jobs to keep enough money coming in to support the family and look after the elephants. He was a farmer, a trekking guide, a mahout (elephant trainer) and also a Muay Thai fighter. He had been in a fight the night before, and although he had won he was heavily bandaged around his legs and stomach which were bruised and swollen. We stopped a few times on the way to his house, mostly for more bandages and painkillers.

We were part of a group of nine that day, which included Swiss, German, French, Catalan and us Brits. We started by changing in to our blue mahout outfits, partly to protect our own clothes as we would be getting soaked and dirty today, and also to help the elephants recognise us. Wood then got us all to tell him our names, and he wrote them in Thai in blue marker pen on our hands. We then had to write down and memorise the eight Thai commands we would be using that day to guide the elephants. Forwards, backwards, left, right, slowly and stop once we were on them, but before that two commands which got the elephant to lift its leg so you could step on it, before raising it higher so you could then climb onto its back.

The elephants were across the road from Wood’s house and for the time being, chained to the ground so they couldn’t roam far. Wood explained that they were in the process of building a fence around a large section of their land so the elephants could be unchained, but until that was completed, there was too much risk of them grazing on other farmers crops which would put them in danger of attack or more likely, pesticide infection. On the way I had to carry a large bundle of sugar-cane which would give us all chance to feed each elephant and them chance to learn our smell.

The first elephant we fed gave us all a ’kiss’ afterwards, which was actually a slimy suck on the neck from her trunk. We moved around the enclosure feeding all of the elephants masses of sugarcane. Each one eats roughly three-to-four-hundred kilos of food each day, so when they aren’t asleep they are almost always eating.

We then had to put our commands in to practice and climb up on the elephants; once Wood was satisfied we moved on to guiding them left, right and practising forwards and backwards. Difficult to describe how it felt having this huge animal listening to your commands and (most of the time) doing what you asked. Once the group had completed this stage we all headed back over to the house for a lunch of curry and omelette while the elephants had a chance to rest. Wood explained more about the daily routine they go through as keepers of the elephants and then it was time to take them for a walk.

The journey was split in two and with two people on each elephant we would switch at the half-way point. I lead the way to start with whilst Nat held on to a loop of rope around the elephants stomach behind me. We slowly climbed high up a hill on Wood’s land and stopped off at an elevated hut. On the way the elephants would occasionally ignore any commands and just head for the nearest patch of vegetation, tearing huge sections of it with their trunk and munching on it as they walked on. At the hut we feed them more sugar-cane and gave them chance to have a rest and scratch themselves against the huts legs. Wood showed us how to make a fire with a piece of bamboo and also how to turn a section in to a water bottle. For the journey back down Nat and I swapped and she was the mahout. I then realised just how difficult it was to hold on to the rope and get comfortable sitting on the elephants back rather than its neck.

![](/thebigtrip/assets/images/2010/11/18093578-IMG_0606.JPG)

We stopped off next to a stream at the bottom of the hill and after dismounting, the elephants all rushed in to the water and led down to cool off. We were all given brushes to wade in and scrub the elephants which they seemed to enjoy a lot. With a few commands unknown to us, the group of guides got the elephants to start spraying us with water and we had a water fight which we were always going to lose. It was then time to take them for a swim in a lake nearby. This time it was three to an elephant, with Nat and I and a mahout all sat up on the elephant whilst it started its swim around the lake. Every so often the mahout would call out a command and the elephant would dive down under water, submerging us to our necks before it rose up again with its head above water. They all seemed to be really enjoying this time in the water, something they also do first thing in the morning when they are brought down before any tours arrive. Fun and games over we made our way back up to the house and it was time to head back in to Chiang Mai.

That evening the we had some great food at SK2 before walking down to the Mae Nam Ping river. During the three day festival the Thai people light hundreds of floating balloon lanterns and place floating flower arrangements with incense and candles on the river as an offering. The river is very important to their faith as it washes away bad luck and brings good. We bought a krathong and with the help of some locals who had a lighter, set our own offering on the Mae Nam Ping. This evening there were more lanterns in the sky than stars. The lighting of the lanterns isn’t supposed to begin until after 8pm due to the close proximity of Chiang Mai airport, but it didn’t seem the Thai people were too concerned about it as the sky was filled with them long before then.

**Sunday 21st November**

We woke up to the realisation we had been away nine months! We had a very relaxed day planned and after grabbing some breakfast, returned to the guest house to lay on the sun terrace for as long as we could bear it. That turned out to be not very long as it was suffocating hot. After no more than an hour we were downstairs and taking a dip in the swimming pool. We relaxed in the restaurant and along with a few drinks caught up with the online world. Our host, Nick, gave us free whisky, which as Nat isn’t a fan of I got to drink. In the evening we ventured out to the night market to have another look for a charm for Nat’s bracelet, but still undecided we left empty-handed.

**Monday 22nd November**

Up early we grabbed a bite to eat and awaited our pick-up. Today we were going on a Thai cooking course. We were taken by sawngthaew (a small pick-up with a bench down each side of the trailer) to the school before being whisked toward the local market by the course leader Tock Tack. We got to see all the different vegetables, herbs and spices we would be cooking with, and picked up what we would be needing for our days cooking. We had seven things to prepare and Nat and I chose different dishes so we could feast on as many different ones as possible.

We first prepared a Thai soup and salad, then a curry paste to go with a curry and a stir-fry. Finally we finished with spring rolls and a dessert. Throughout we were praised with Tock Tack offering us a ’delicious’ or ’beautiful’ to keep us enthused. At the beginning I was a little concerned about the size of each dish, but after eating six meals I was satisfied I wouldn’t need any more food that day. Included with the course was a book containing all the recipes, so we will be testing them out on you when we get home!

**Tuesday 23rd November**

It was time to escape the bounds of the old city and head for Wat Phra That Doi Suthep, a huge temple fifteen kilometres from Chiang Mai, on a hill looking back over the city. We hired a scooter for the day and set off, a little unsure of the exact direction to be heading. After going around in circles for twenty minutes and ending up at Chiang Mai Zoo, we asked one of the staff there where to go and got some much needed directions. Back on the right road we soon left the hustle of the main roads in the city and began a winding climb up the hill toward Doi Suthep. We knew we had arrived when the road narrowed and was squeezed between stalls either side selling tourist tat and Thai fast food. We parked up and began our climb up the many steps to the temple entrance. Once at the top we checked we were ‘foreigners’ and concluded we had to buy a ticket for entry.

The temple itself was grand and had many different shrines and buildings, each full of images of Buddha. We admired the view back across the city and stopped for a coffee and bite to eat at a café within the temple walls. We witnessed a monk blessing a family and many locals putting donations in every single donation box they could find. Back outside the temple walls Nat finally found her elephant charm in a collection of stalls selling everything under the sun.

Nick had told us on the way out of SK2 that morning, to carry on up the winding road after the temple to the palace and lastly a village tribe on the top of the hill. We dutifully followed his advise but on arrival at the palace realised it had already closed for the day. Venturing further on we kept climbing until it was actually quite cold riding the scooter, only to start descending down the other side of the hill. After ten more minutes we decided we must have taken another wrong turn and headed back.

That evening we went back to the man on the street cooking Pad Thai before lots more planning of our route through Asia. We were gutted to concede that we didn’t have enough time to see everything we wanted, and as a result decided to drop Vietnam from our planned route.

**Wednesday 24th November**

It would rank up there alongside the day we missed our flight to Singapore from Sydney. Nothing was going right. We stopped in at an Air Asia office in Chiang Mai to see how we claim back some of the taxes from the missed connecting flights from Singapore on to Chiang Mai. It turns out we needed to become members first and then contact a call centre for more help. We dropped the scooter back at the rental shop and then headed to an internet café to make a bunch of unsatisfying phone calls to Virgin Atlantic, Air New Zealand and Singapore Airlines. We spent ages on the phone trying to get someone to change our future flights on to India and then finally to London, being spun around in circles as each airline was having difficulty doing anything with the ticket due to its shared nature. Eventually Singapore Airlines confirmed they had changed our flight to India but we had to give up on Virgin sorting the final leg home as they had to pass it on to another team who weren’t customer facing.

We staggered back in to SK2 in foul moods and weren’t looking forward to our night bus to the Laos border, the beginning of our two day trip to Luang Prabang. Our saviour of the day was Nick, who along with letting us use his phone free of charge, cooked us up huge quantities of delicious food and threw in a free dessert. For the bus ride he gave us bananas to take with us, and after a few beers we were feeling back to normal. It made leaving all the harder when at 9pm our minibus arrived. We promised to recommend his ’home’ to everyone we knew coming to Chiang Mai (as was how we heard of it from Katie) and said goodbye to the staff. Even the scary, permanently drunk guy from the kitchen with the complete face tattoo was there smiling and waving us goodbye.

The bus ride was thankfully four and a half hours rather than the promised six. Some spirited driving from the driver, combined with some terrible roads and us perched on the back seats above the rear-axle left Nat feeling travel sick most of the way though. In front of us was a very chatty Englishman by the name of Ben, who had decided the best way to cope with the journey was to drink as much rum as possible and listen to Black Sabbath at full volume on his iPod. We arrived in the border town of Chiang Khong at 1:30am, where we stumbled, unorganised in to a bedroom with the alarm set for 7am.
