Our minibus to Vang Vieng was an hour late getting to us and then it turned out this was only the bus to the bus station, we would be changing when we got there. On arrival we found out we were the last two to get on the bus, so as usual we got the back seats. Mine was actually just in front of Nat and was a fold out seat with a broken backrest. It was going to be an uncomfortable six hours. It was improved by absolutely stunning scenery though.

The journey was broken in to two hour chunks with food and toilet stops carefully arranged at friendly but remote and expensive places. At one point we had to stop in traffic and could see an accident up ahead. As it was baking hot we all got out for a bit and I walked up the hill to see what was going on. An articulated lorry had failed to make a turn down the winding hill and had crashed off the side of the road. It had rolled at some point and was a crumpled mess. The worst accident we have seen so far on our trip. No sign of the driver so we could only hope he had made it out ok. It wasn’t much longer before we started to see small concrete signs revealing the remaining distance to Vang Vieng.

Arriving at the Vang Vieng bus terminal we were still two kilometres from the main strip so we hopped in a tuk-tuk and got dropped on the main drag. We were hoping to bump in to our friend Solaug (nicknamed ‘Soup’ in Byron Bay as we couldn’t pronounce her Norwegian name properly) and decided to check for availability at the guesthouse she had mentioned. We got a room overlooking the Nam Song River and after dumping our gear headed out for a look around. You can walk from one end of Vang Vieng to the other in around fifteen minutes, which we did whilst hunting for some food. Vang Vieng is exactly like the guide book says; lots of identical bars, serving identical food and all running back-to-back episodes of ‘Friends’ on their TV‘s. We did manage to find one without a TV which we decided to grace with our presence and had some food whilst people watching as drunken, spray-painted river-revellers returned with their yellow inflatable rings. It was eerily calm though; we were expecting carnage and a clash of music from tightly packed bars but it was very chilled out. Could it be that everyone is drunk and passed out by 6pm?

The next morning we grabbed breakfast at a restaurant with a very slightly different view of the Nam Song before getting our gear together for a day of tubing. We stuffed the camera and some cash in to a dry-bag and went to pay for our tubes. Grouped in with five other ‘tubers’ and squashed in to a waiting tuk-tuk with all the rings tied to the top, we made the three kilometre journey north, where the first of the bars is built on the river. Hopping out of the tuk-tuk we were force fed lao-lao whisky before crossing a bridge to Q-Bar, our first drinking hole. After we were given wrist-bands granting us free shots at each of the bars and Nat’s back had been spray-painted we grabbed a beer and looked on as people at a bar slightly down-river on the other side swung from a huge trapeze/rope-swing. There was also a zip-line and a couple of water-slides. It was clear that this section of river was like the village’s evil twin. This was the Vang Vieng we were expecting. The bars were well filled with people already and as others gently bobbed along in the water, bar staff through lines out to them so they could be dragged ashore.

We zigzagged our way down the river, stopping off for the occasional free whisky and to watch people flying around on the swings and zip-lines. It all looked like far too much fun and we soon joined in, trying to avoid swallowing too much river water when falling in. The biggest swing was at our last bar stop, named The Slide Bar as it also boasted the biggest water slide on the river. More of a ski jump than a water slide, we watched as people flew off the end of it, usually landing flat on their backs as they hit the water, surfacing with a look of excitement mixed with pain. We had a few goes on the swing as it was easily the best thing on the river. The take-off platform must have been forty feet above the water below and you swung alongside the bar and past the slide exit before choosing your dismount point.

From there we jumped back in our rings and floated off down-river towards the main village. It turned out to be another two kilometres on the way back and the sun had set by the time we got back to our guesthouse. We had much needed showers and wandered out to look for food and bumped in to Soup at the restaurant next-door. She had been in Vang Vieng for five days and was leaving for Bangkok the following day. We had a few more beers and caught up with her before surrendering to the need for sleep after a day of acrobatics and much paddling.

We had decided to just do the one full day in Vang Vieng as the tubing was the main attraction, and although you could probably do it more than once (we heard of one guy who had done it nineteen days in a row) we thought it best to move on to the capital city of Vientiane. We packed up our gear and went for some breakfast before chilling at the guesthouse and trying to use the very slow internet connection to catch-up with online goings-on. At around 1pm our bus (that would take us to the bus station) arrived and we got the back seat again. But this time the back seat had obviously broken so badly it had been removed and replaced with a plank of wood. Luckily our bus to Vientiane was waiting at the bus station and after helping the driver load all our bags on to the roof, I grabbed an ice-cream from an opportunistic vendor and we were on our way in comfy seats to the big smoke, Vientiane.
